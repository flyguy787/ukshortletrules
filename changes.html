Changes log

What has changed in UK short-let rules?

This log lists every change to short-term let and holiday let rules in England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland that the UK Short-Let Rules team has recorded: licensing, planning, visitor levies, council tax premiums and registration. Each entry links to its official source. There are 52 entries so far, the latest dated 28 Oct 2026; see the council pages or the visitor levy map for the rules in force now.

Each council page also has its own dated What changed list and the date its rules were last checked. Start at all councils.

October 2026

  • 28 Oct 2026 Scotland Confirmed from Highland Council: on 28 Sep 2026 its Economy and Infrastructure Committee agreed to submit two proposed short-term let control areas to Scottish Ministers, Inverness City and Highland Rural (several rural areas, including Aird and Loch Ness, Lochaber, Skye and Raasay, and Sutherland). Not in force until Ministers decide.
    Council page: Highland · Official source
  • 28 Oct 2026 Wales Confirmed from the official minutes: Isle of Anglesey full Council kept the 100% second-home council tax premium for 2026/27 on 5 Mar 2026.
    Council page: Isle of Anglesey · Official source
  • 28 Oct 2026 England Confirmed from the official minutes: Brighton & Hove Budget Council approved serviced accommodation as an exception to the second homes council tax premium on 26 Feb 2026, in force from 1 Apr 2026.
    Council page: Brighton & Hove · Official source
  • 28 Oct 2026 England Liverpool's Submission Draft Local Plan 2043 (Regulation 19) keeps Policy TC7 on short-term lets: converting a home would not be supported where 10% or more of properties within 100m are HMOs or where it would over-concentrate short-term lets, and a management plan would be required. Not yet examined or adopted.
    Council page: Liverpool · Official source
  • 28 Oct 2026 England Liverpool's consultation on a replacement selective landlord licensing scheme (current scheme ends March 2027) closed on 5 Oct 2026; no decision published yet. Selective licensing covers rented homes, not holiday lets.
    Council page: Liverpool · Official source
  • 27 Oct 2026 Wales Now tracked: the Development of Tourism and Regulation of Visitor Accommodation (Wales) Act 2026 (2026 asc 10) received Royal Assent on 27 April 2026. It sets up licensing by the Welsh Ministers for self-contained self-catering lets, with standards on fire safety, electrical and gas checks, carbon monoxide alarms and public liability insurance. The licensing duty and offences are not in force: the legal default start is 4 March 2030, after consultation on regulations (fees are not set yet). Registering with the Welsh Revenue Authority by 31 March 2027 is a separate requirement.
    Official source
  • 27 Oct 2026 Wales The Welsh Government has extended its consultation on the 182-day self-catering rule for local tax, and the 5 proposed exemptions, to 11:59pm on Fri 30 Oct 2026. It was due to close at 11:59pm on 23 Oct but the form closed early at 5pm. Email responses sent after 5pm on 23 Oct will still be counted. The current rules still apply.
    Official source
  • 26 Oct 2026 Wales Re-checked the Wales 182-day business rates rule against GOV.UK guidance and the Non-Domestic Rating (Amendment of Definition of Domestic Property) (Wales) Order 2026 (WSI 2026/7). Both confirm that from 1 April 2026 a holiday let that wasn't let for 182 nights in the last 12 months can still qualify if its average nights let over the previous 2 or 3 years is at least 182, and that up to 14 nights a year donated to a registered charity (for free use by its beneficiaries) count. The Wales page row is marked verified. The old GOV.WALES self-catering business rates page now returns 404, so we link to the GOV.UK guidance instead.
    Official source
  • 26 Oct 2026 Scotland Added the published short-term let licence fees for Glasgow (from April 2026: secondary letting grant £310 for up to 4 guests or £494 for 5 or more), Dundee (£310 to £3,100 by guest capacity, same for every licence type) and Fife (£295 to £826 for a full licence by occupancy). Our licence fee finder now lists fees for all 32 Scottish councils.
    Council pages: Dundee City, Fife, Glasgow City · Official source
  • 26 Oct 2026 England Added council pages for Torbay (Torquay, Paignton and Brixham) and Brighton & Hove. Both charge a 100% second homes council tax premium, so 200% council tax, from 1 Apr 2025. Holiday lets valued for business rates instead do not pay it. Neither has a short-let licence or an Article 4 direction on holiday lets; Brighton & Hove says some short lets may need planning permission.
    Council pages: Brighton & Hove, Torbay · Official source
  • 26 Oct 2026 England Added council pages for Bath and North East Somerset and New Forest (Hampshire). Both charge a 100% second homes council tax premium, so 200% council tax, from 1 Apr 2025, and still in 2026/27. Holiday lets valued for business rates instead do not pay it. Neither has a short-let licence or an Article 4 direction on holiday lets; the New Forest National Park's draft Local Plan proposes principal residence conditions on new homes.
    Council pages: Bath and North East Somerset, New Forest · Official source
  • 26 Oct 2026 England Added council pages for West Devon (Tavistock and Okehampton) and Teignbridge (Newton Abbot, Teignmouth and Dawlish). Both charge a 100% second homes council tax premium, so 200% council tax, from 1 Apr 2025, and still in 2026/27. Holiday lets valued for business rates instead do not pay it. Neither has a short-let licence or an Article 4 direction on holiday lets; Teignbridge's new Local Plan requires new-build homes in five coastal parishes to be primary residences, and Dartmoor National Park conditions new holiday lets to holiday use only.
    Council pages: Teignbridge, West Devon · Official source
  • 26 Oct 2026 England Added council pages for City of York and Derbyshire Dales (Matlock, Bakewell and Ashbourne). Both charge a 100% second homes council tax premium, so 200% council tax, from 1 Apr 2025. Holiday lets valued for business rates instead do not pay it. Neither has a short-let licence or an Article 4 direction on holiday lets; York has draft planning guidance on short-term holiday lets awaiting approval, and the York and North Yorkshire visitor levy report is now on both York and North Yorkshire pages.
    Council pages: City of York, Derbyshire Dales, North Yorkshire · Official source
  • 26 Oct 2026 England Added council pages for Bournemouth, Christchurch and Poole (BCP) and the Isles of Scilly. Both charge a 100% second homes council tax premium, so 200% council tax, from 1 Apr 2025; the Isles of Scilly adds a local exception for staff accommodation. Holiday lets valued for business rates instead do not pay it. Neither has a short-let licence or an Article 4 direction on holiday lets. BCP Council agreed a short-term lets motion on 12 May 2026 (use existing powers, map lets, gather evidence for a possible future Article 4 direction); the Isles of Scilly Local Plan resists turning permanent homes into holiday lets. The visitor levy map now shows the BCP Accommodation BID charge as void (ballot declared void in Jan 2025).
    Council pages: Bournemouth, Christchurch and Poole, Isles of Scilly · Official source
  • 25 Oct 2026 Wales Added council pages for Blaenau Gwent and Merthyr Tydfil, so all 22 Welsh councils now have a page. Merthyr Tydfil keeps its 100% second-home council tax premium for 2026/27 (full Council, 11 Feb 2026). Blaenau Gwent's own page says a 100% second-home premium starts from April 2026, but its 2026/27 return to the Welsh Government shows none, so we mark it unverified and suggest checking with the council. Neither has a visitor levy or an Article 4 direction on short lets; WRA registration still applies by 31 Mar 2027.
    Council pages: Blaenau Gwent, Merthyr Tydfil · Official source
  • 25 Oct 2026 Wales Added council pages for Caerphilly (100% second-home council tax premium since April 2025, still charged in 2026/27; long-term empty premium only after 2 years, up to 300% after 5) and Torfaen (no second-home premium: one of only two Welsh councils not charging one in 2026/27, though the council says it is considering premiums subject to consultation). Neither has a visitor levy or an Article 4 direction on short lets; WRA registration still applies by 31 Mar 2027.
    Council pages: Caerphilly, Torfaen · Official source
  • 25 Oct 2026 Wales Added council pages for Neath Port Talbot (100% second-home council tax premium since April 2025, plus a new 6-month premium exception from April 2026 for self-catering lets moved off business rates onto council tax) and Rhondda Cynon Taf (100% second-home premium since April 2024). Neither has a visitor levy or an Article 4 direction; WRA registration still applies by 31 Mar 2027.
    Council pages: Neath Port Talbot, Rhondda Cynon Taf · Official source

    Council pages: Bridgend, Newport · Official source

    Council pages: Monmouthshire, Vale of Glamorgan · Official source
