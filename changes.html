Changes log

What has changed in UK short-let rules?

This log lists every change to short-term let and holiday let rules in England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland that the UK Short-Let Rules team has recorded: licensing, planning, visitor levies, council tax premiums and registration. Each entry links to its official source. There are 43 entries so far, the latest dated 26 Oct 2026; see the council pages or the visitor levy map for the rules in force now.

DateNationChangeSource
2026-10-26WalesRe-checked the Wales 182-day business rates rule against GOV.UK guidance and the Non-Domestic Rating (Amendment of Definition of Domestic Property) (Wales) Order 2026 (WSI 2026/7). Both confirm that from 1 April 2026 a holiday let that wasn't let for 182 nights in the last 12 months can still qualify if its average nights let over the previous 2 or 3 years is at least 182, and that up to 14 nights a year donated to a registered charity (for free use by its beneficiaries) count. The Wales page row is marked verified. The old GOV.WALES self-catering business rates page now returns 404, so we link to the GOV.UK guidance instead.source
2026-10-26ScotlandAdded the published short-term let licence fees for Glasgow (from April 2026: secondary letting grant £310 for up to 4 guests or £494 for 5 or more), Dundee (£310 to £3,100 by guest capacity, same for every licence type) and Fife (£295 to £826 for a full licence by occupancy). Our licence fee finder now lists fees for all 32 Scottish councils.source
2026-10-25WalesAdded council pages for Blaenau Gwent and Merthyr Tydfil, so all 22 Welsh councils now have a page. Merthyr Tydfil keeps its 100% second-home council tax premium for 2026/27 (full Council, 11 Feb 2026). Blaenau Gwent's own page says a 100% second-home premium starts from April 2026, but its 2026/27 return to the Welsh Government shows none, so we mark it unverified and suggest checking with the council. Neither has a visitor levy or an Article 4 direction on short lets; WRA registration still applies by 31 Mar 2027.source
2026-10-25WalesAdded council pages for Caerphilly (100% second-home council tax premium since April 2025, still charged in 2026/27; long-term empty premium only after 2 years, up to 300% after 5) and Torfaen (no second-home premium: one of only two Welsh councils not charging one in 2026/27, though the council says it is considering premiums subject to consultation). Neither has a visitor levy or an Article 4 direction on short lets; WRA registration still applies by 31 Mar 2027.source
2026-10-25WalesAdded council pages for Neath Port Talbot (100% second-home council tax premium since April 2025, plus a new 6-month premium exception from April 2026 for self-catering lets moved off business rates onto council tax) and Rhondda Cynon Taf (100% second-home premium since April 2024). Neither has a visitor levy or an Article 4 direction; WRA registration still applies by 31 Mar 2027.source
2026-10-25WalesAdded council pages for Bridgend (second-home council tax premium rose to 200%, a triple bill, from April 2026, among the highest in Wales) and Newport (100% second-home premium since April 2025, which the council says can include holiday lets on council tax). Neither has a visitor levy or an Article 4 direction; WRA registration still applies by 31 Mar 2027.source
2026-10-25WalesAdded council pages for Wrexham (50% second-home council tax premium, one of the lowest in Wales, unchanged since 2017) and Flintshire (100% second-home premium since April 2023, kept for 2026/27). Neither has a visitor levy or an Article 4 direction; WRA registration still applies by 31 Mar 2027.source
2026-10-25WalesAdded council pages for Monmouthshire (100% second-home council tax premium, unchanged for 2026/27; a property moving from business rates to council tax is free of the premium for 12 months) and Vale of Glamorgan (100% second-home premium reaffirmed for 2026/27). Neither has a visitor levy or an Article 4 direction; WRA registration still applies by 31 Mar 2027.source
2026-10-25ScotlandAdded council pages for North Lanarkshire (one licence fee for all types: £315 up to 4 guests, £472.50 for 5 or more; levy feasibility work under way, no consultation yet) and East Dunbartonshire (3-year fee £594 for a secondary let up to 10 guests, same to renew; council decided in 2026 not to introduce a visitor levy). North Lanarkshire has its own levy map row; East Dunbartonshire is shown as 'No levy: decided against'.source
2026-10-24ScotlandAdded council pages for East Renfrewshire (licence fees by bedrooms, £972 to £1,293 for a new secondary let; no visitor levy proposed) and South Lanarkshire (2026/27 fees by guests, £381.43 to £953.58 for a secondary let, same fee to renew; visitor levy research under way, no formal consultation yet). East Renfrewshire added to the levy map's 'None proposed yet' group; South Lanarkshire has its own row.source
2026-10-24ScotlandAdded council pages for Inverclyde (2026/27 licence fees from £508 for a secondary let; levy decision on hold while the council monitors the national rollout) and Renfrewshire (2026/27 fees by bedrooms, £927 to £1,274 for a secondary let; renewal fee not published; levy proposition being developed). Both added to the levy map's 'None proposed yet' group.source
2026-10-24ScotlandAdded council pages for Midlothian (2026/27 licence fees from £541 for a secondary let; levy to be reassessed in 2026) and West Lothian (planning permission or a certificate of lawfulness needed with every licence application; two conflicting fee tables flagged). Both added to the levy map's 'None proposed yet' group.source
2026-10-24EnglandAdded council pages for Liverpool and Sefton (second homes pay double council tax since 1 April 2025; holiday lets exempt from selective landlord licensing; Sefton short-term holiday lets planning guidance; Liverpool City Region Overnight Visitor Levy not yet in force).source
2026-10-24ScotlandAdded council pages for East Ayrshire (new secondary let licence from £250; visitor levy paused until Aug 2027) and Clackmannanshire (2026/27 fees from £514.30 for a secondary let; no levy proposed). East Ayrshire moved to the 'paused' row on the levy map.source
2026-10-24EnglandAdded council pages for East Suffolk and Somerset (100% second homes council tax premium since 1 April 2025; East Suffolk gives homes for sale up to 24 months' exception; no licensing scheme).source
2026-10-24ScotlandAdded a council page for North Ayrshire, including Arran and Cumbrae (licence £300 or £480 for a new secondary let from 1 April 2026; no control area; visitor levy not yet decided).source
2026-10-24ScotlandAdded a free "Is your licence renewal due?" checker for Scottish short-term let licences: estimates your expiry date and when to start your renewal, with renewal rules from the 1982 Act, Scottish Government guidance and council pages (Highland, Edinburgh, Argyll and Bute, Na h-Eileanan Siar, Angus).source
2026-10-24ScotlandAdded council pages for South Ayrshire (visitor levy paused until August 2027 at the earliest; licensing policy and fees under review) and Angus (licence £349 to £588, renewals now up to 6 years; no control area), with licence fees and levy status.source
2026-10-24ScotlandAdded 4 Scottish council pages: Falkirk (5% visitor levy consultation open to 25 Nov 2026), Orkney Islands, Shetland Islands and Na h-Eileanan Siar (Western Isles), with 2026/27 licence fees and levy status. The three island councils are not pursuing an overnight levy.source
2026-10-23England/WalesAdded 8 council pages for holiday-let hotspots: North Norfolk, Dorset, South Hams, North Devon, Isle of Wight (100% second homes premium from 1 Apr 2025), Denbighshire (150%), Carmarthenshire (100%) and Swansea (100%), plus a free licence and registration checker.source
2026-10-23WalesWelsh Government consultation on how self-catering properties are classified for local tax (including the 182-day letting threshold and 5 proposed exemptions) closed. No response or decision published yet; current rules still apply.source
