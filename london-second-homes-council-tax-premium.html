London second homes council tax premium by borough

Do London boroughs charge the second homes premium?

Yes. All 33 London councils now charge a 100% second homes premium, so a furnished home that is nobody's main home pays double council tax. 30 were already charging it in 2025-26, according to their October 2025 returns to MHCLG, and Barking and Dagenham, Hillingdon and Kensington and Chelsea started on 1 April 2026. In October 2025, 30,786 London second homes were paying it, 10,587 of them in Westminster. Holiday lets valued for business rates do not pay it.

At a glance

  • 33 of 33 London councils have a 100% premium (the most an English council can charge): 30 charged it in 2025-26, the first year English councils could, and 3 started on 1 April 2026.
  • 30,786 second homes in London were paying it in October 2025, 18% of the 170,299 in England. Most: Westminster (10,587), Tower Hamlets (3,231), Camden (2,286), Hammersmith and Fulham (2,160), City of London (1,809).
  • 7,952 more London second homes were not charged because an exception applied (October 2025), most in Tower Hamlets (4,318), Wandsworth (1,180) and Haringey (1,080).
  • A Band D second home pays £2,056.42 (Wandsworth) to £5,218.40 (Kingston upon Thames) a year in 2026-27.

What it means for London short lets

  • Your own home, let while you are away: still your main home, so no second homes premium. The 90-night rule still limits whole-home short letting without planning permission.
  • A furnished flat that is nobody's main home, on council tax: the borough charges 200% council tax unless an exception fits.
  • Valued for business rates: a let available at least 140 nights and actually let at least 70 nights in the last 12 months is valued for business rates, so it pays no council tax premium (GOV.UK).
  • Exceptions: Classes E to L, including annexes, homes marketed for sale or let (12 months), probate and planning-restricted holiday homes (MHCLG guidance). See the England second homes premium guide.

Common questions

Do all London boroughs charge the second homes premium?

Yes, all 33 London councils (the 32 boroughs and the City of London) have now decided to charge it, at 100%. In October 2025, 30 of them told MHCLG they were charging it in 2025-26. Barking and Dagenham, Hillingdon and Kensington and Chelsea started on 1 April 2026; each has said or decided its premium is 100%.

How much is council tax on a second home in London?

A 100% premium doubles the bill. Using MHCLG's average Band D figures for 2026-27 (including the Greater London Authority precept), a Band D second home pays from £2,056.42 a year in Wandsworth to £5,218.40 in Kingston upon Thames. Other bands are a fixed share of Band D, from 6/9 for Band A to 18/9 for Band H.

Does my London short let pay the second homes premium?

It can. If the property is furnished and nobody's sole or main home, and it is on council tax, the council can charge the premium unless an exception applies. If you let your own home while you are away, it is still your main home, so the premium does not apply. A holiday let valued for business rates (available at least 140 nights and actually let at least 70 nights in the last 12 months) pays business rates instead of council tax, so no premium.

Which exceptions could stop the premium?

The national exceptions (Classes E to L) include annexes treated as part of the main home, homes actively marketed for sale or let (up to 12 months), recent probate (up to 12 months), job-related homes, and seasonal homes where a planning condition limits use to holiday accommodation or stops year-round living. Apply to your borough with evidence.

Which London council has the most second homes paying the premium?

Westminster, by a long way: 10,587 second homes were being charged the premium in October 2025, about 34% of London's 30,786. Next were Tower Hamlets (3,231), Camden (2,286) and Hammersmith and Fulham (2,160).

Where do the numbers come from?

Each council reports how many second homes it charges to MHCLG every October (Council Taxbase statistics). The 2025 release was published on 6 November 2025 and revised on 21 January 2026. Band D amounts come from MHCLG's Council Tax levels for 2026-27 (25 March 2026). We will update this page when the 2026 Council Taxbase release is out.
