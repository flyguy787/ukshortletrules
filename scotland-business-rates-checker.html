Does my Scottish holiday let pay business rates or council tax?

Enter your nights for one financial year (1 April to 31 March) to see whether your self-catering property in Scotland should be on the non-domestic rates roll, or will be charged council tax instead.

One short, sourced email a week.

Not legal or tax advice. The local Assessor decides. Always check the official sources below.

The rules in brief

  • To be rated as self-catering holiday accommodation, the property must not be anyone's only or main residence, be available to let commercially for 140+ nights and actually let for 70+ nights in the financial year (1 April to 31 March).
  • You must prove it to the Assessor every year: booking calendars and adverts (availability), and booking dates and income per booking (lettings). Explain any booking over 28 nights. If you self-advertise, include bank statements.
  • Deadline: evidence must reach the Assessor before 27 May (i.e. by 26 May) after the year ends. If the Assessor asks after the year ends, you have 56 days from the request. Late or insufficient evidence means the property goes on the council tax list for that year.
  • Council tax on a second home: since 1 April 2024 councils can charge a premium of up to 100% (double). A second home is furnished, not anyone's main home and lived in for at least 25 days a year. 29 of 32 councils announced the full premium for 2024 (not Falkirk, Glasgow or North Ayrshire; North Ayrshire has charged it since 1 April 2025); check your council for its current policy.
  • You also need a short-term let licence, whichever tax applies.

Official sources

  • Scottish Assessors: self-catering properties (evidence and declaration)
  • Scottish Assessors: evidence required for 2025-26 (news, 23 Jun 2026)
  • Legislation: Council Tax (Dwellings and Part Residential Subjects) (Scotland) Regs 1992, reg 5A (evidence deadline)
  • gov.scot: council tax on second homes (premium up to 100% from 1 Apr 2024)
  • gov.scot: Finance circular 6/2024, second and empty homes
