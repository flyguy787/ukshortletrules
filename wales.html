Wales short-let rules: quick answers

Do I need to register my holiday let with the Welsh Revenue Authority?

In force. From 1 Oct 2026, anyone taking bookings of 31 nights or less must register with the Welsh Revenue Authority by 31 Mar 2027. Registration is free and applies even where no levy exists.

Is there a visitor levy (tourist tax) on short-term lets in Wales?

Council-by-council. £1.30 per person per night (75p for hostels, dorms and tent pitches). The earliest start is 1 Apr 2027. Cardiff starts 1 Apr 2027. Councils must give 12 months' notice.

Do I need a licence to run a short-term let in Wales?

Legislated, not in force. A licensing scheme for self-contained self-catering lets has Royal Assent. Its legal default commencement is March 2030, with consultation on regulations first.

What is the 182-day rule for holiday lets in Wales?

In force (verified). To be rated as a business, a let must have been available commercially for at least 252 nights and actually let for at least 182 nights in a 12-month period, and you must intend to make it available for 252 nights in the next 12 months. Stays over 28 nights don't count towards the 182. From 1 Apr 2026 (Non-Domestic Rating (Amendment of Definition of Domestic Property) (Wales) Order 2026, WSI 2026/7) you can meet the 182 nights as an average over the previous 2 or 3 years, and up to 14 nights a year donated to a registered charity count. Otherwise council tax applies, and councils can charge a second-home premium (premium cap UNVERIFIED this session).

Is the 182-day rule in Wales changing?

Consultation closes 23 Oct 2026. The Welsh Government is consulting (31 Jul to Fri 23 Oct 2026) on the criteria for classifying self-catering properties for local tax, including whether the 182-day letting threshold is right and 5 proposed exemptions (properties that are part of a wider business, large multi-unit properties, properties with a relevant planning restriction, properties within the curtilage of the owner's home, and properties on the owner's farm). Nothing has changed yet.

Do I need planning permission for a short-term let in Wales?

In force. Since Oct 2022 Wales has use classes C3 (main home), C5 (second home) and C6 (short-term let). Changes between them are permitted development unless an Article 4 direction applies.
