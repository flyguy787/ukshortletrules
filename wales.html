Wales

Free registration with the Welsh Revenue Authority is mandatory by 31 March 2027. Councils can start a visitor levy (£1.30 per person per night); only Cardiff has so far. Licensing is legislated for, with a default start of March 2030. Business rates need 182 days let.

Wales short-let rules: quick answers

Do I need to register my holiday let with the Welsh Revenue Authority?

In force. From 1 Oct 2026, anyone taking bookings of 31 nights or less must register with the Welsh Revenue Authority by 31 Mar 2027. Registration is free and applies even where no levy exists.

Is there a visitor levy (tourist tax) on short-term lets in Wales?

Council-by-council. £1.30 per person per night (75p for hostels, dorms and tent pitches). The earliest start is 1 Apr 2027. Cardiff starts 1 Apr 2027. Councils must give 12 months' notice. Isle of Anglesey decided on 24 Sep 2026 not to introduce a levy at present, and Conwy deferred any decision the same day. Gwynedd says it has not decided yet. GOV.WALES lists Cardiff as the only council charging the levy and no councils currently consulting.

Do I need a licence to run a short-term let in Wales?

Legislated, not in force. A licensing scheme for self-contained self-catering lets has Royal Assent. Its legal default commencement is March 2030, with consultation on regulations first.

What is the 182-day rule for holiday lets in Wales?

In force (verified). To be rated as a business, a let must have been available commercially for at least 252 nights and actually let for at least 182 nights in a 12-month period, and you must intend to make it available for 252 nights in the next 12 months. Stays over 28 nights don't count towards the 182, but those nights can count towards the 252 available nights if the property could have been booked for 28 nights or less (GOV.UK guidance, corrected 9 Oct 2026). From 1 Apr 2026 (Non-Domestic Rating (Amendment of Definition of Domestic Property) (Wales) Order 2026, WSI 2026/7) you can meet the 182 nights as an average over the previous 2 or 3 years, and up to 14 nights a year donated to a registered charity count. Otherwise council tax applies, and councils can charge a second-home premium of up to 300% of the standard council tax (cap raised from 100% for 2023-24 onwards by WSI 2022/370; each council decides whether to charge one and at what level, and statutory exceptions apply).

Is the 182-day rule in Wales changing?

Consultation open until 30 Oct 2026. The Welsh Government is consulting (from 31 Jul 2026) on the criteria for classifying self-catering properties for local tax, including whether the 182-day letting threshold is right and 5 proposed exemptions (properties that are part of a wider business, large multi-unit properties, properties with a relevant planning restriction, properties within the curtilage of the owner's home, and properties on the owner's farm). It was due to close at 11:59pm on Fri 23 Oct 2026 but closed early at 5pm that day, so the Welsh Government has extended it to 11:59pm on Fri 30 Oct 2026. Email responses sent after 5pm on 23 Oct will still be counted. Nothing has changed yet, so the current rules still apply. We'll update this when the outcome is published.

Do I need planning permission for a short-term let in Wales?

In force. Since Oct 2022 Wales has use classes C3 (main home), C5 (second home) and C6 (short-term let). Changes between them are permitted development unless an Article 4 direction applies.
