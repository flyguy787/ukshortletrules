Short-term let rules in Derbyshire Dales (updated 26 Oct 2026)

Last checked 26 Oct 2026: no change logged for this council in our changes log. Get free alerts when it changes.

100% council tax premium on second homes (200% bill) since 1 Apr 2025, with national and local exceptions. Holiday lets that qualify for business rates (140 nights available, 70 let) pay business rates instead. No licensing or local registration scheme.

Licensing or registration

None found

No licensing or local registration scheme. A national short-term let register for England is coming under the Levelling-up and Regeneration Act 2023; GOV.UK (updated 2 Oct 2026) says it is expected to begin in March 2027. Until then, the main local rules are council tax or business rates, planning, and fire safety.

Control area / planning

None found

No short-let control area or Article 4 direction for holiday lets found (searched 26 Oct 2026). The C5 short-term let use class is still only a proposal in England, so whether letting a whole home is a material change of use is decided case by case by the planning authority. Part of the district is in the Peak District National Park, where the National Park Authority handles planning. Check before converting a home or outbuilding. The national planning data register lists no Article 4 directions for Derbyshire Dales District Council, and 26 for the Peak District National Park Authority on individual sites (farms, quarries, land uses and building works), none on holiday lets in houses (checked 26 Oct 2026). Inside the National Park, Development Management Policy DMR3 (adopted May 2019) says that outside the settlements named in its Core Strategy, new self-catering accommodation, where acceptable, is restricted to holiday use; the Authority's planning reports describe this as holiday accommodation for no more than 28 days a year by any one person. Inside those settlements, a holiday occupancy condition is only used where a converted building has too little living space or is too close to neighbours to be a permanent home. The Authority's draft Local Plan (Preferred Approach, September 2025) proposes a primary residence clause on all new housing, including holiday accommodation converted to housing, citing AirDNA figures of 7,035 short-let listings in the Peak District area in August 2025. It is not adopted and would not apply to existing homes.

Fees

No short-let fees. Council tax: the council announced on 4 Mar 2024 that it had approved a 100% second homes premium from 1 Apr 2025, and its annual budget meeting on 3 Mar 2025 unanimously confirmed it, so a furnished home that is nobody's main home pays double council tax. Exceptions (national rules from 1 Apr 2025) include: annexes treated as part of the main home (Class F); homes actively marketed for sale or let (Classes G and H, 12 months); probate (Class I, 12 months); job-related homes (Class J); occupied caravan pitches and boat moorings (Class K); and seasonal homes where year-round living is banned, or a planning condition limits use to holiday accommodation or stops anyone staying more than 28 days in a row (Class L). Derbyshire Dales adds local exceptions from 1 Apr 2025: armed forces members whose main home near their work is not provided by their employer, and age-restricted homes being actively marketed for sale or rent (up to a further 12 months after the national exception ends). From 1 Apr 2026, rural huts in council tax band A with no water or energy supply and no waste collection are also excepted. The premium ends if the home becomes your main home, is sold, or is let to someone as their main home; tell the revenues team (01629 818222), which may arrange a visit. Empty and unfurnished homes pay a premium after 2 years: 100% (2 to 5 years), 200% (5 to 10 years) and 300% (10 years or more), and the 2-year period does not restart when the owner changes. If your holiday let meets the business rates test (available at least 140 nights and actually let at least 70 nights in the last 12 months, and you plan to offer 140 nights in the next 12), it is valued for business rates instead of council tax, so the premium does not apply. A single let with a rateable value under £15,000 may get small business rate relief.
