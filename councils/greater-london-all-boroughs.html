Fees

No short-let fees. Council tax is set by each of London's 33 billing authorities (32 boroughs and the City of London).

Second homes premium: all 33 now charge the 100% premium (double council tax) that English councils can charge from 1 Apr 2025. On 6 Oct 2025, 30 of the 33 were charging it, according to the councils' returns to MHCLG (Council Taxbase 2025).

Westminster had the most homes paying it (10,587), then Tower Hamlets (3,231), Camden (2,286) and Hammersmith and Fulham (2,160). The other three started on 1 Apr 2026, all at 100%: Barking and Dagenham, Kensington and Chelsea, and Hillingdon (approved by Full Council on 22 Jan 2026). See every borough, with second homes paying and the Band D bill on a second home. Exceptions (national rules from 1 Apr 2025) include: annexes treated as part of the main home (Class F); homes actively marketed for sale or let (Classes G and H, 12 months); probate (Class I, 12 months); job-related homes (Class J); occupied caravan pitches and boat moorings (Class K); and seasonal homes where year-round living is banned, or a planning condition limits use to holiday accommodation or stops anyone staying more than 28 days in a row (Class L).

If your holiday let meets the business rates test (available at least 140 nights and actually let at least 70 nights in the last 12 months, and you plan to offer 140 nights in the next 12), it is valued for business rates instead of council tax, so the premium does not apply.

A single let with a rateable value under £15,000 may get small business rate relief.

What changed in Greater London (all boroughs)

  • 5 Nov 2026 Rules on this page last checked in full against the official sources listed above.
  • 3 Nov 2026 All England lets Confirmed two Budget 2026 details for holiday lets. The higher Small Business Rate Relief thresholds from 1 April 2027 (100% relief up to £18,000 rateable value, tapering to £21,000) cover self-catering holiday lets that pay business rates, because they use the retail, hospitality and leisure definition, which includes self-catering accommodation and holiday homes. And mortgage interest relief for let property is given at the property basic rate under Finance Act 2026, so it rises to 24% from 6 April 2027 once Finance Bill 2026-27 passes. Official source
