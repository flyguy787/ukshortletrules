Short-term let rules in East Suffolk (updated 22 Oct 2026)

Last checked 22 Oct 2026: no change logged for this council in our changes log. Get free alerts when it changes.

100% council tax premium on second homes (200% bill) since 1 Apr 2025, with homes for sale exempt for up to 24 months. Holiday lets that qualify for business rates (140 nights available, 70 let) pay business rates instead. No licensing or local registration scheme.

Fees

No short-let fees. Council tax: Full Council approved in Feb 2024 a 100% premium on homes that are furnished and nobody's main home, so 200% council tax from 1 Apr 2025. Council tax is billed by Anglia Revenues Partnership for East Suffolk. Exceptions (national rules from 1 Apr 2025) include: annexes treated as part of the main home (Class F); homes actively marketed for sale or let (Classes G and H, 12 months); probate (Class I, 12 months); job-related homes (Class J); occupied caravan pitches and boat moorings (Class K); and seasonal homes where year-round living is banned, or a planning condition limits use to holiday accommodation or stops anyone staying more than 28 days in a row (Class L). East Suffolk goes further than the national rules: a home actively marketed for sale is excepted for up to 24 months (not 12), and the Section 151 Officer can waive the premium in exceptional cases such as vulnerability or hardship. Claim an exception through the online form, choosing the Long Term Empty and Second Homes option. If your holiday let meets the business rates test (available at least 140 nights and actually let at least 70 nights in the last 12 months, and you plan to offer 140 nights in the next 12), it is valued for business rates instead of council tax, so the premium does not apply. A single let with a rateable value under £15,000 may get small business rate relief.
