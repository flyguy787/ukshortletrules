Licensing or registration

Applies

Licence needed (Scotland-wide). All short-term lets (secondary letting, home letting, home sharing) need a licence from the council before taking bookings or guests. Step-by-step licence guide and checklist. Apply and pay online on fife.gov.uk. Fife says processing can take up to 9 months, a full licence usually lasts up to 3 years, and operating without one can mean a fine of up to £2,500 and a one-year ban from applying.

Control area / planning

None found

No control area. mygov.scot lists only two in Scotland (all of Edinburgh, and Badenoch & Strathspey in Highland).

Fife Council consulted on the principle of designating control areas (16 Jun to 8 Sep 2025). Any designation would need Scottish Ministers' approval.

Reports that the council has since chosen St Andrews and the East Neuk for a control area: UNVERIFIED.

Visitor levy

None found

No scheme approved. Fife ran early engagement surveys (latest closed 31 Jan 2026, adding a flat-rate option).

Any draft scheme would need a formal consultation, then at least 18 months before it starts, so the council says 2028 is the earliest. Reports of an Oct 2026 decision to move to formal consultation: UNVERIFIED.

Fees

Fee table "as of February 2025" in the council's short-term let policy (updated Dec 2025), by occupancy, children under 2 in a cot not counted. Full licence, first or renewal (same fee): £295 (1 to 2 guests), £354 (3 to 4), £413 (5 to 6), £472 (7 to 8), £531 (9 to 10), £590 (11 to 12), then £59 per extra 1 to 2 guests up to £826 (19 to 20).

Temporary licence £246 to £777; temporary exemption £195 to £200. Variation or transfer £75; visit after a complaint or breach up to £282.

Fees are non-refundable. The policy says fees are reviewed and may change, so confirm on the council's page.
