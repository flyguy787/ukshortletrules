Short-term let rules in Isle of Anglesey (updated 22 Oct 2026)

What changed (24 Sep 2026): Isle of Anglesey decides not to introduce a visitor levy at present; Conwy defers any decision. Cardiff remains the only Welsh council charging the levy. Source · Get alerts

No visitor levy: Full Council decided on 24 Sep 2026 not to introduce one at present. Council tax second-home premium kept at 100% for 2026-27 (so a second home pays double).

Licensing or registration

Registration required

No council licence yet. Wales-wide: if you take bookings of 31 nights or fewer you must register with the Welsh Revenue Authority by 31 Mar 2027 (free, applies even with no council levy). A national licensing scheme is legislated but not in force (default commencement March 2030). Check if you must register.

Fees

WRA registration is free. Council tax second-home premium: 100% on top of the standard charge (so 200% in total). The Executive's 2026-27 budget proposal confirmed it is maintained at 100%; the council's second-homes page still shows older wording. Final adoption by full Council on 5 Mar 2026: UNVERIFIED. Exceptions include Class 5 (occupied caravan pitches and boat moorings) and Class 6 (seasonal homes or holiday lets where year-round occupation is prohibited, for example by a planning condition).
