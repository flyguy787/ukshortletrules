Control area / planning

None found

No short-let control area, and no Article 4 direction aimed at holiday lets (checked 3 Nov 2026). The national planning data register lists 4 Article 4 directions for Oxford: Jericho (2011), Osney Town (1993), Wolvercote Green (2007) and a city-wide direction for houses in multiple occupation (from 25 Feb 2012); none is about holiday lets.

Under the Oxford Local Plan 2036 (adopted 8 June 2020), Policy V5 allows new holiday and other short stay accommodation only in the city centre, district centres, sites allocated for it and on the main arterial roads with frequent, direct public transport to the city centre, and only with no loss of a residential dwelling; Policy H5 refuses development that causes the net loss of a self-contained home, with narrow exceptions.

In a decision of 11 Dec 2024 a planning inspector upheld the council's enforcement notice against a house in Headington run as short-term let accommodation, finding a material change of use from a C3 home that conflicted with Policies H5 and V5; the owner had 12 weeks to stop its primary use as a short-term let.

The inspector made clear that occasional short-term letting of a house that stays mainly a home may not need permission: it is a question of fact and degree in each case. A new Oxford Local Plan 2045 was submitted for examination on 14 July 2026; stage 2 hearings run from 24 Nov to 11 Dec 2026 and the council expects to adopt it in May 2027.

We have not checked its short stay policy wording (unverified), and it is not yet in force.

Fees

No short-let fees. Council tax: from 1 Apr 2025 a 100% premium applies to most second homes (furnished homes that are nobody's sole or main residence), so they pay double council tax (200%).

Exceptions (national rules from 1 Apr 2025) include: annexes treated as part of the main home (Class F); homes actively marketed for sale or let (Classes G and H, 12 months); probate (Class I, 12 months); job-related homes (Class J); occupied caravan pitches and boat moorings (Class K); and seasonal homes where year-round living is banned, or a planning condition limits use to holiday accommodation or stops anyone staying more than 28 days in a row (Class L).

If one applies, use the council's online form to apply for an exception (you need your 9-digit council tax account number). Empty and unfurnished homes have paid premiums since 1 Apr 2024: 100% after 1 year, 200% after 5 years and 300% after 10 years, and a change of owner does not reset the clock.

If your holiday let meets the business rates test (available at least 140 nights and actually let at least 70 nights in the last 12 months, and you plan to offer 140 nights in the next 12), it is valued for business rates instead of council tax, so the premium does not apply.

A single let with a rateable value under £15,000 may get small business rate relief.
