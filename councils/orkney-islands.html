Licence needed (£275 to £583 for 3 years, 25% off on non-linked isles). No control area. Council decided against an overnight visitor levy on 23 Sep 2025.

Licensing or registration

Applies

Licence needed (Scotland-wide). All short-term lets (secondary letting, home letting, home sharing) need a licence from the council before taking bookings or guests. Step-by-step licence guide and checklist. Apply through Orkney Islands Council: guidance notes, application form, floor and location plans, and a separate Scottish Fire and Rescue Service fire safety checklist. You display a notice at the property (28-day objection period) only when the council asks you to. The council publishes a register of licences.

Source: source source

🏘

Control area / planning

None found

No control area. mygov.scot lists only two in Scotland (all of Edinburgh, and Badenoch & Strathspey in Highland).

Source: source

💷

Visitor levy

None found

No levy. The council's Policy and Resources Committee decided on 23 Sep 2025 not to go ahead with an overnight visitor levy "at this time", after a feasibility study with Shetland and the Western Isles found strong local opposition, practical problems and marginal returns. The council prefers a cruise ship or point-of-entry levy, which the current law does not allow.

Source: source

💰

Fees

3-year licence, 2026/27 fees (3.8% up on 2025/26). Secondary letting: £354 (up to 4 guests), £434 (5 to 9), £583 (10 or more). Home sharing and home letting: £275 / £343 / £457. Non-linked isles get 25% off: secondary £266 / £326 / £437, home sharing and letting £206 / £257 / £343. Temporary licence (up to 6 weeks) £275 (£206 non-linked isles), transfer £138 (£103). The first £275 of each application fee is non-refundable.
