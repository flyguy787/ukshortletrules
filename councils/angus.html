📋

Licensing or registration

Applies

Licence needed (Scotland-wide). All short-term lets (secondary letting, home letting, home sharing) need a licence from the council before taking bookings or guests. Step-by-step licence guide and checklist. Apply through Angus Council's short-term lets pages. A new licence lasts up to 3 years and a renewal up to 6 years.

If you started letting after 1 Oct 2022 you cannot trade until licensed, and the council has 9 months to decide. A temporary licence lasts up to 6 weeks, and you can get up to three temporary exemptions totalling no more than 6 weeks in 12 months.

The council keeps a public register of short-term lets.

🏘

Control area / planning

None found

No control area. mygov.scot lists only two in Scotland (all of Edinburgh, and Badenoch & Strathspey in Highland). Angus Council confirms there are currently no short-term let control areas in Angus, and says that could change.

Source: source source

💷

Visitor levy

None found

No levy. In June 2024 the Policy and Resources Committee said it was not making any decision to introduce a levy and asked officers to engage with stakeholders first.

In October 2025 the council set aside £50,000 of UK Shared Prosperity funding for a tourism framework and early engagement on a possible levy. There is no published scheme or consultation yet, and any Scottish levy needs at least 18 months' notice.

Source: source source

💰

Fees

Same fee for all licence types, set by guest numbers: £349 (1 to 5 guests), £460 (6 to 20), £588 (more than 20) for a new 3-year licence or a 6-year renewal. Provisional licence 20% of the full fee (£69.80 / £92 / £117.60), confirmation 80% (£279.20 / £368 / £470.40).

Temporary licence or temporary exemption £174.50, variation £75, transfer £75, duplicate £20. No refund if refused or granted for a shorter period.

The April 2026 licensing fee rise did not cover short-term lets, so these still apply.

Source: source source
