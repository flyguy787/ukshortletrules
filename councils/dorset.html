Short-term let rules in Dorset (updated 22 Oct 2026)

100% council tax premium on second homes (200% bill) since 1 Apr 2025. Holiday lets that qualify for business rates (140 nights available, 70 let) pay business rates instead. No licensing or local registration scheme.

Licensing or registration

No licensing or local registration scheme. A national short-term let register for England is coming under the Levelling-up and Regeneration Act 2023; GOV.UK (updated 2 Oct 2026) says it is expected to begin in March 2027. Until then, the main local rules are council tax or business rates, planning, and fire safety.

Control area / planning

No short-let control area or Article 4 direction for holiday lets found (checked 23 Oct 2026). The C5 short-term let use class is still only a proposal in England, so whether letting a whole home is a material change of use is decided case by case by the planning authority. Check before converting a home or outbuilding.

Visitor levy

No visitor levy. On 10 Sep 2026 the government announced that mayors and Foundation Strategic Authority leaders in England will get the power to bring in an Overnight Visitor Levy, as a percentage of the accommodation price. A bill is still to come, so nothing applies yet.

Fees

No short-let fees. Council tax: Dorset Council agreed at its Feb 2024 budget meeting to charge a 100% premium on second homes (furnished, nobody's only or main home) from 1 Apr 2025, so 200% council tax. The premium applies from day one; there is no minimum period as a second home. Exceptions (national rules from 1 Apr 2025) include: annexes treated as part of the main home (Class F); homes actively marketed for sale or let (Classes G and H, 12 months); probate (Class I, 12 months); job-related homes (Class J); occupied caravan pitches and boat moorings (Class K); and seasonal homes where year-round living is banned, or a planning condition limits use to holiday accommodation or stops anyone staying more than 28 days in a row (Class L). Apply for an exception with evidence to secondhomes@dorsetcouncil.gov.uk and keep paying your bill while it is considered. The council also considers hardship reductions under s13A. If your holiday let meets the business rates test (available at least 140 nights and actually let at least 70 nights in the last 12 months, and you plan to offer 140 nights in the next 12), it is valued for business rates instead of council tax, so the premium does not apply. A single let with a rateable value under £15,000 may get small business rate relief.
