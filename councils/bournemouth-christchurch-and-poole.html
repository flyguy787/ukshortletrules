Short-term let rules in Bournemouth, Christchurch and Poole (updated 26 Oct 2026)

Last checked 26 Oct 2026: no change logged for this council in our changes log. Get free alerts when it changes.

100% council tax premium on second homes (200% bill) since 1 Apr 2025, with national exceptions. Holiday lets that qualify for business rates (140 nights available, 70 let) pay business rates instead. No licensing or local registration scheme; in May 2026 the council agreed to look at using existing powers on short lets and gather evidence for a possible future Article 4 direction.

Licensing or registration

None found

No licensing or local registration scheme. A national short-term let register for England is coming under the Levelling-up and Regeneration Act 2023; GOV.UK (updated 2 Oct 2026) says it is expected to begin in March 2027. Until then, the main local rules are council tax or business rates, planning, and fire safety. On 12 May 2026 Full Council unanimously backed a motion on short-term lets (minute 111): the council will look at how environmental health, planning enforcement, anti-social behaviour and licensing powers can tackle nuisance from short lets, map short-term lets, promote reporting routes for residents, start preparing for the national register, and ask to join any government pilot on regulating short lets. None of this creates a local licence or registration scheme yet.

Control area / planning

None found

No short-let control area or Article 4 direction for holiday lets (searched 26 Oct 2026). BCP's Article 4 directions on houses in multiple occupation (all of the Bournemouth area since December 2011, and Talbot Village in Poole since 1 May 2013) cover changing a house into a small HMO, not holiday lets, and the national planning data register lists none for the council. Whether letting a whole home is a material change of use is decided case by case. The motion Full Council passed on 12 May 2026 asks officers to investigate whether homes let for more than 90 nights a year are a material change of use, to gather evidence that could support an Article 4 direction in future, and to use the new Local Plan to set policies on short-term lets. Nothing has been decided: the new BCP Local Plan is at an early stage (the council's timetable shows consultation on a proposed plan from September 2027 and indicative adoption in April 2029). In the Bournemouth area, the Tourist Accommodation Supplementary Planning Document (adopted April 2016 by the former Bournemouth Borough Council) aims to keep existing tourist accommodation and says new self-catering or serviced holiday flats are acceptable where the holiday use is secured by condition or legal agreement. Check with the planning team before converting a home or outbuilding.

Visitor levy

None found

No visitor levy. On 10 Sep 2026 the government announced that mayors and Foundation Strategic Authority leaders in England will get the power to bring in an Overnight Visitor Levy, as a percentage of the accommodation price. A bill is still to come, so nothing applies yet. In Bournemouth, Christchurch and Poole, larger hotels in a proposed Accommodation Business Improvement District voted for a charge of £2 per room per night (ballot result published by the council on 14 May 2024), but the government declared the ballot void in January 2025 after some eligible businesses did not get ballot papers, so no charge is collected (as reported by the Bournemouth Echo). It would not have applied to typical holiday lets anyway. In December 2025 the council said BCP is not in a strategic mayoral authority area and asked the government to let areas like it use the new overnight visitor levy; in June 2026 the government said it had consulted on this and would respond in due course (as reported by BBC News).

💰

Fees

No short-let fees. Council tax: Full Council on 9 Jan 2024 (59 votes to 3, with 3 abstentions) approved the formal determination, following its decision on 12 Jul 2022 to charge a second homes premium. The council's page says the premium was introduced from 1 Apr 2025, so a furnished home that is nobody's main home pays 200% council tax. There is no discount for a furnished home that is not your main home or is between lets, unless it is needed for your job. Exceptions (national rules from 1 Apr 2025) include: annexes treated as part of the main home (Class F); homes actively marketed for sale or let (Classes G and H, 12 months); probate (Class I, 12 months); job-related homes (Class J); occupied caravan pitches and boat moorings (Class K); and seasonal homes where year-round living is banned, or a planning condition limits use to holiday accommodation or stops anyone staying more than 28 days in a row (Class L). BCP's operational guidance lists only these national exceptions, with no extra local ones. You must send evidence, for example listings on sales or letting websites with the date marketing started; the marketing exception applies only once per taxpayer, and the council expects to be told of changes within 21 days. In cases of exceptional hardship you can apply for a discretionary reduction (section 13A). Empty and unfurnished homes pay a premium after 1 year (since 1 Apr 2024): the bill is 200% after 1 year, 300% after 5 years and 400% after 10 years. If your holiday let meets the business rates test (available at least 140 nights and actually let at least 70 nights in the last 12 months, and you plan to offer 140 nights in the next 12), it is valued for business rates instead of council tax, so the premium does not apply. A single let with a rateable value under £15,000 may get small business rate relief. The Valuation Office Agency, not the council, decides whether a let moves to business rates.
