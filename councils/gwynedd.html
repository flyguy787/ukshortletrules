Licensing or registration

Registration required

No council licence yet. Wales-wide: if you take bookings of 31 nights or fewer you must register with the Welsh Revenue Authority by 31 Mar 2027 (free, applies even with no council levy).

A national licensing scheme is legislated but not in force (default commencement March 2030). Check if you must register · What the licensing Act will require.

Control area / planning

Applies

Two planning authorities cover Gwynedd. Inside Eryri National Park, the park authority's Article 4 direction has been in force since 1 Jun 2025: planning permission is needed to change a main home (C3) into a second home (C5) or short-term holiday let (C6), or to switch between C5 and C6.

Homes already used as a second home or holiday let before 1 Jun 2025 are not affected, and the park authority suggests keeping evidence of that use (for example tax or letting records). Outside the park, Cyngor Gwynedd's own Article 4 direction was quashed by the High Court and is not in force; the Court of Appeal refused permission to appeal on 6 Feb 2026, so these changes are permitted development there (checked 1 Nov 2026).

Visitor levy

None found

No levy. The council consulted from 20 May to Jul 2026 and says (page updated 1 Oct 2026) it has not made a decision. Any Gwynedd levy could start no earlier than Oct 2027. GOV.WALES lists Cardiff as the only council charging.

Fees

WRA registration is free. Council tax second-home premium: 150% on top of the standard charge (250% in total) since 1 Apr 2023.

Cyngor Gwynedd first charged 50% from 1 Apr 2018, then 100% from 1 Apr 2021. On 4 Dec 2025 the council kept the 150% premium, with no discount, for 2026/27, and raised the long-term empty homes premium (empty 12 months or more) from 100% to 150%.

Its 2026/27 return to the Welsh Government shows 3,758 second homes paying the 150% premium. Premium exceptions: Classes 1 and 2 (marketed for sale or let, one year only), Class 3 (annexes), Class 4 (armed forces), Class 5 (occupied caravan pitches and boat moorings), Class 6 (seasonal homes where year-round occupation is prohibited) and Class 7 (job-related homes).

A self-catering let valued for business rates (available at least 252 nights and let at least 182) pays no council tax, so no premium.
