Short-term let rules in Powys (updated 22 Oct 2026)

Last checked 22 Oct 2026: no change logged for this council in our changes log. Get free alerts when it changes.

Council tax second-home premium of 75% (175% of the standard bill), in place since 1 Apr 2023 and unchanged for 2026-27. No visitor levy at this stage; any levy left to the council elected in May 2027, earliest start 2028.

Licensing or registration

Registration required

No council licence yet. Wales-wide: if you take bookings of 31 nights or fewer you must register with the Welsh Revenue Authority by 31 Mar 2027 (free, applies even with no council levy). A national licensing scheme is legislated but not in force (default commencement March 2030). Check if you must register · What the licensing Act will require.

Control area / planning

None found

No Article 4 direction or local short-let planning control found on the council's site (checked 23 Oct 2026). In the Brecon Beacons (Bannau Brycheiniog) National Park, the park authority handles planning.

💷

Visitor levy

None found

No levy at this stage. Full Council voted 29 to 19 on 9 Oct 2025 not to implement a levy for now and to run an engagement exercise. That survey closed 30 Sep 2026 and results go to Cabinet and full Council. The council says any decision would be for the administration elected in May 2027, a formal consultation would be needed, and 2028 is the earliest a levy could start.

Source: source source

💰

Fees

WRA registration is free. Council tax second-home premium: 75% on top of the standard charge (175% in total), effective since 1 Apr 2023 and unchanged for 2026-27. The council's 2026-27 tax base report notes 235 extra self-catering units moved from business rates into council tax after failing the Welsh 252/182-day letting test. Exceptions include Class 5 (occupied caravan pitches and boat moorings) and Class 6 (seasonal homes or holiday lets where year-round occupation is prohibited, for example by a planning condition).
