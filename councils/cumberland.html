Short-term let rules in Cumberland (updated 22 Oct 2026)

Last checked 22 Oct 2026: no change logged for this council in our changes log. Get alerts when it changes.

100% council tax premium on second homes (200% bill) since 1 Apr 2025. Holiday lets that qualify for business rates (140 nights available, 70 let) pay business rates instead. No licensing or local registration scheme.

📋

Licensing or registration

None found

No licensing or local registration scheme. A national short-term let register for England is coming under the Levelling-up and Regeneration Act 2023; GOV.UK (updated 2 Oct 2026) says it is expected to begin in March 2027. Until then, the main local rules are council tax or business rates, planning, and fire safety.

No short-let control area or Article 4 direction for holiday lets found on the council site (checked 23 Oct 2026). Planning is decided case by case; inside the Lake District National Park, the park authority handles planning. Check before converting a home or outbuilding.

Fees

No short-let fees. Council tax: on 6 Mar 2024 the council adopted a 100% premium on furnished homes that are nobody's main home, so 200% council tax is payable from 1 Apr 2025 (before then, second homes paid 100%). Exceptions (national rules from 1 Apr 2025) include: annexes treated as part of the main home (Class F); homes actively marketed for sale or let (Classes G and H, 12 months); probate (Class I, 12 months); job-related homes (Class J); occupied caravan pitches and boat moorings (Class K); and seasonal homes where year-round living is banned, or a planning condition limits use to holiday accommodation or stops anyone staying more than 28 days in a row (Class L). You must apply for an exception, and apply in the area where the second home is. For the marketing exception, the same owner can only use the sale exception once, and the letting exception again only after the home has been let for at least six months in a row. A job-related second home may get a 50% discount. If your holiday let meets the business rates test (available at least 140 nights and actually let at least 70 nights in the last 12 months, and you plan to offer 140 nights in the next 12), it is valued for business rates instead of council tax, so the premium does not apply. A single let with a rateable value under £15,000 may get small business rate relief.
