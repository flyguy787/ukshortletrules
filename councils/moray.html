Licensing or registration

Applies

Licence needed (Scotland-wide). All short-term lets (secondary letting, home letting, home sharing) need a licence from the council before taking bookings or guests. Step-by-step licence guide and checklist. Email the application to licensing@moray.gov.uk (or post it to the Licensing Team, Council Offices, High Street, Elgin IV30 1BX) and pay the full fee at the same time; the council will not process it until everything is in.

Moray does not grant temporary exemptions; it may grant a temporary licence of up to six weeks. Each property needs its own licence.

Control area / planning

None found

No control area. mygov.scot lists only two in Scotland (all of Edinburgh, and Badenoch & Strathspey in Highland).

Moray says you must check whether you need planning permission (or a certificate of lawfulness) before applying for a licence, and if you do, apply for it first. Provisional licences need the relevant planning certificate.

Visitor levy

None found

No decision. Consultation on whether Moray should introduce a visitor levy at all opened 2 Sep 2026 and runs to 31 Oct 2026.

If the council goes ahead, a draft scheme would get a further public consultation before any final decision. Separately, second homes pay a 100% council tax premium (double council tax).

The council is consulting until 14 December 2026 on raising it, and the long-term empty homes charge, now that councils can set more than 100%; the consultation does not consider lowering them.

Fees

From 1 Apr 2026: licence or renewal £540 (same for new and existing operators); temporary licence £540; provisional licence £220; confirmation of a provisional licence £320; transfer £309; variation £30; copy of licence £24; inspection £220.

Planning, Building Standards and Environmental Health charge separately. Fees are not refunded if an application is refused.
