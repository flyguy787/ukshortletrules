Short-term let rules in Moray (updated 22 Oct 2026)

Licence needed (£540 new or renewal from 1 Apr 2026). No control area. Consultation on whether to have a visitor levy at all runs to 31 Oct 2026; no decision made.

Licensing or registration

Licence needed (Scotland-wide). All short-term lets (secondary letting, home letting, home sharing) need a licence from the council before taking bookings or guests. Step-by-step licence guide and checklist. Email the application to licensing@moray.gov.uk (or post it to the Licensing Team, Council Offices, High Street, Elgin IV30 1BX) and pay the full fee at the same time; the council will not process it until everything is in. Moray does not grant temporary exemptions; it may grant a temporary licence of up to six weeks. Each property needs its own licence.

Source: source source

Control area / planning

No control area. mygov.scot lists only two in Scotland (all of Edinburgh, and Badenoch & Strathspey in Highland). Moray says you must check whether you need planning permission (or a certificate of lawfulness) before applying for a licence, and if you do, apply for it first. Provisional licences need the relevant planning certificate.

Source: source source

Visitor levy

No decision. Consultation on whether Moray should introduce a visitor levy at all opened 2 Sep 2026 and runs to 31 Oct 2026. If the council goes ahead, a draft scheme would get a further public consultation before any final decision.

Source: source

Fees

From 1 Apr 2026: licence or renewal £540 (same for new and existing operators); temporary licence £540; provisional licence £220; confirmation of a provisional licence £320; transfer £309; variation £30; copy of licence £24; inspection £220. Planning, Building Standards and Environmental Health charge separately. Fees are not refunded if an application is refused.
