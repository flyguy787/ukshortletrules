Licensing or registration

None found

No licensing or local registration scheme. A national short-term let register for England is coming under the Levelling-up and Regeneration Act 2023; GOV.UK (updated 2 Oct 2026) says it is expected to begin in March 2027.

Until then, the main local rules are council tax or business rates, planning, and fire safety.

Control area / planning

None found

No short-let control area or Article 4 direction aimed at holiday lets (checked 2 Nov 2026). The national planning data register lists 12 older Article 4 directions for the borough (1952 to 1997), on sites such as the coastal areas at Heacham and Snettisham, Holme next the Sea, Old Hunstanton and the King's Lynn and New Houghton conservation areas; none is about holiday lets.

Whether letting a whole home as a holiday let is a material change of use is decided case by case. The King's Lynn and West Norfolk Local Plan 2021-2040 (adopted 27 Mar 2025) Policy LP09 says new holiday accommodation permitted in the open countryside will get occupancy conditions: holiday use only, made available to rent as commercial holiday lets, no more than 28 days per let, never anyone's sole or main residence, and a lettings register kept for the council to inspect.

Several neighbourhood plans go further. The Heacham Neighbourhood Plan (made 2022) Policy 4 says new open-market homes, other than replacement dwellings, must be restricted to use as a principal residence, and new unrestricted second homes will not be supported.

The Burnham Market Neighbourhood Plan 2022-2036 (made 26 Sep 2023, after 80.1% backed it at referendum) Policy 3 requires new homes to be the occupants' sole or main residence, secured by condition and a section 106 agreement, and says changing a home to a furnished holiday let will not be supported where planning permission is needed.

25 neighbourhood plans were in force in the borough by November 2025, so check the one for your parish.

Fees

No short-let fees. Council tax: Full Council agreed in January 2024 to charge a 100% second homes premium from 1 Apr 2025, re-approved it in January 2025 and again in July 2025 for 2026/27, and reviews it each year.

A furnished home that is nobody's main home pays double council tax; the council says there are more than 3,000 second homes in West Norfolk (about 1 in 20 homes). Exceptions (national rules from 1 Apr 2025) include: annexes treated as part of the main home (Class F); homes actively marketed for sale or let (Classes G and H, 12 months); probate (Class I, 12 months); job-related homes (Class J); occupied caravan pitches and boat moorings (Class K); and seasonal homes where year-round living is banned, or a planning condition limits use to holiday accommodation or stops anyone staying more than 28 days in a row (Class L).

The council adds local exceptions: a planning condition that stops the home being anyone's sole or main residence also counts (as well as holiday-only and 28-day conditions), and a furnished home let on an assured shorthold tenancy to people living there as their main home is excepted for the days it is empty between lets.

There is no right of appeal against the decision to charge the premium. Empty and unfurnished homes pay an extra 100% after 1 year (200% bill), 200% after 5 years (300% bill) and 300% after 10 years (400% bill); an uninhabitable home under major repair may get a 25% discount for up to 12 months.

If your holiday let meets the business rates test (available at least 140 nights and actually let at least 70 nights in the last 12 months, and you plan to offer 140 nights in the next 12), it is valued for business rates instead of council tax, so the premium does not apply.

A single let with a rateable value under £15,000 may get small business rate relief.
