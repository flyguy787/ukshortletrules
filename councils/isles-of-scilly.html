Short-term let rules in Isles of Scilly (updated 26 Oct 2026)

Last checked 26 Oct 2026: no change logged for this council in our changes log. Get free alerts when it changes.

100% council tax premium on second homes (200% bill) since 1 Apr 2025, with national exceptions and a local staff accommodation exception. Holiday lets that qualify for business rates (140 nights available, 70 let) pay business rates instead. No licensing or local registration scheme. The Local Plan resists losing permanent homes to holiday lets.

Control area / planning

None found

No short-let control area or Article 4 direction aimed at holiday-let use (searched 26 Oct 2026). The national planning data register lists four Article 4 directions for the Council of the Isles of Scilly (1975, 1988, 1995 and 1998); they remove permitted development rights for house extensions and alterations, temporary uses of land, agricultural works, swimming pools, temporary buildings, and roof, window, door and painting changes in the Conservation Area, so many works to a holiday let need planning permission even where they would not elsewhere. The whole of the islands is a Conservation Area and a National Landscape (AONB). The Isles of Scilly Local Plan 2015-2030 (adopted 25 Mar 2021) is strict on holiday lets: Policy WC5 says converting a property for tourism must not lose homes that would otherwise be available for permanent occupation unless there are wider public benefits, and an informal bed and breakfast or extra holiday letting accommodation within the curtilage of a home will not be permitted unless a certificate of lawful use as a C1 guesthouse has been obtained; Policy LC8 says a replacement dwelling, extension or alteration must not include any self-contained holiday letting accommodation unless this is already lawful; Policy LC1 says any open-market homes allowed to fund affordable housing will be conditioned as principal residence homes; and Policy LC2 says all new affordable homes must be occupied as the principal residence of someone with a local housing need. The council has used section 106 agreements to restrict who can occupy new homes since at least the 1990s, so check the title of any property for an occupancy restriction. A Local Plan review is under way (timetable approved by Full Council on 26 Feb 2026). Talk to the planning team (planning@scilly.gov.uk) before letting or converting a property.

Fees

No short-let fees. Council tax: on 22 Feb 2024 Full Council approved a budget that included bringing in premiums for empty homes and second homes, and the council's Empty Homes and Second Homes Premium Policy 2026-27 confirms a 100% premium on second homes from 1 Apr 2025, so a furnished home that is nobody's main home pays double council tax. Exceptions (national rules from 1 Apr 2025) include: annexes treated as part of the main home (Class F); homes actively marketed for sale or let (Classes G and H, 12 months); probate (Class I, 12 months); job-related homes (Class J); occupied caravan pitches and boat moorings (Class K); and seasonal homes where year-round living is banned, or a planning condition limits use to holiday accommodation or stops anyone staying more than 28 days in a row (Class L). The council adds a local exception for staff accommodation: no premium where a local employer owns or rents the home for its staff, or where someone employed by an island business rents it and keeps their main home elsewhere in the UK. Second homes of people who must live elsewhere for their job may get a 50% discount. To claim an exception contact revenues@scilly.gov.uk or 0300 1234 105 (option 1) with evidence; hardship linked to a protected characteristic can be considered for a discretionary reduction (section 13A), and appeals go to the Valuation Tribunal. Empty and unfurnished homes pay a 100% premium after 12 months (from 1 Apr 2025), and may get a 50% discount for up to six months first. If your holiday let meets the business rates test (available at least 140 nights and actually let at least 70 nights in the last 12 months, and you plan to offer 140 nights in the next 12), it is valued for business rates instead of council tax, so the premium does not apply. A single let with a rateable value under £15,000 may get small business rate relief.
