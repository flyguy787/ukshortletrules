What are the holiday let rules in Ceredigion?

There is no council licence in Ceredigion, but if you take bookings of 31 nights or fewer you must register with the Welsh Revenue Authority by 31 Mar 2027 (free). Second homes and self-catering units that fail the criteria for business rates pay a 150% council tax premium (250% of the standard bill) since 1 Apr 2025. There is no visitor levy, and we found no Article 4 direction or local short-let planning control; fire safety law still applies, see holiday let fire safety in Wales.

Registration required

No council licence yet. Wales-wide: if you take bookings of 31 nights or fewer you must register with the Welsh Revenue Authority by 31 Mar 2027 (free, applies even with no council levy).

A national licensing scheme is legislated but not in force (default commencement March 2030). Check if you must register · What the licensing Act will require.

Fees

WRA registration is free. Council tax second-home premium: 150% on top of the standard charge (250% in total) from 1 Apr 2025 (100% from 1 Apr 2024).

The council says this covers second homes and self-catering units that fail the criteria for business rates. No discount for second homes.

Exceptions include Class 5 (occupied caravan pitches and boat moorings) and Class 6 (seasonal homes or holiday lets where year-round occupation is prohibited, for example by a planning condition). Ceredigion also lists annexes used as part of the main home and homes being marketed for sale or let (up to 12 months).
