Short-term let rules in Ceredigion (updated 22 Oct 2026)

Council tax second-home premium of 150% (250% of the standard bill) since 1 Apr 2025, covering second homes and self-catering units not in business rates. No visitor levy decision found.

Licensing or registration

No council licence yet. Wales-wide: if you take bookings of 31 nights or fewer you must register with the Welsh Revenue Authority by 31 Mar 2027 (free, applies even with no council levy). A national licensing scheme is legislated but not in force (default commencement March 2030). Check if you must register.

Control area / planning

No Article 4 direction or local short-let planning control found on the council's site (checked 23 Oct 2026).

Visitor levy

No levy. We found no council decision or consultation; the council's 2026-27 medium term financial strategy says it has not yet set a formal position on whether to consider charging the levy, and GOV.WALES does not list Ceredigion among councils charging or consulting. Any Welsh levy needs a consultation, a full Council decision and 12 months' notice.

Source: source source

Fees

WRA registration is free. Council tax second-home premium: 150% on top of the standard charge (250% in total) from 1 Apr 2025 (100% from 1 Apr 2024). The council says this covers second homes and self-catering units that fail the criteria for business rates. No discount for second homes. Exceptions include Class 5 (occupied caravan pitches and boat moorings) and Class 6 (seasonal homes or holiday lets where year-round occupation is prohibited, for example by a planning condition). Ceredigion also lists annexes used as part of the main home and homes being marketed for sale or let (up to 12 months).
