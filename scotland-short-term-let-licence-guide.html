How to get a short-term let licence in Scotland: steps and checklist

Every short-term let in Scotland needs a council licence before you take bookings or guests. Here are the steps, and a checklist you can tick off as you go (it saves in your browser).

The steps

  1. Check you need one. Most hosts do. See the exclusions on mygov.scot.
  2. Pick your licence type. Secondary letting, home sharing, home letting, or home letting and home sharing.
  3. Check planning. If you are in a control area (all of Edinburgh, and Badenoch & Strathspey in Highland), letting a whole home that isn't your main home very likely needs planning permission. See Scotland rules.
  4. Get your certificates and safety items in place. Use the checklist below.
  5. Apply through your council's website and pay its fee. Fees vary by council. Example: Highland fees.
  6. Display a public notice at or near the property for 21 days from the date you applied.
  7. Wait for a decision. Councils have up to 9 months.
  8. Once licensed, put your licence number in every advert and display the required information for guests.

Common questions

Do I need a short-term let licence in Scotland?

Yes, if you let accommodation to guests on a temporary basis and no exclusion applies. You must have the licence before you take bookings or receive guests. Exclusions include immediate family, tenancies, hotels with hotel planning permission, caravans on licensed holiday sites, aparthotels and bothies.

Which type of licence do I need?

There are 4 types. Secondary letting is for a place you do not normally live in, such as a holiday cottage. Home sharing is letting all or part of your home while you live there. Home letting is letting your home while you are away. Home letting and home sharing covers both.

How long does a licence last?

A licence usually lasts 3 years.

How long does the council take to decide?

Councils must decide within 9 months, unless the sheriff court gives them more time. If they miss the deadline without an extension, they must give you a licence for up to a year while they finish.

What happens if I let without a licence?

It is a criminal offence. You can be fined up to £2,500 and banned from applying for a licence for a year.

How much does a licence cost?

Each council sets its own fees, based on the property and type of let. Check your council's website.

Can I let for a short time without a full licence?

Some councils offer temporary licences (up to 6 weeks a year) or temporary exemptions (up to 3 in 12 months, no more than 6 weeks in total). It is up to each council. A provisional licence is available if you are building new accommodation.

Can I appeal if I am refused?

Yes, at the sheriff court within 28 days of the decision. You cannot reapply for a year unless your circumstances change materially.
