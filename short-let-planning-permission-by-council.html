Short-let planning permission by council (UK)

Which councils need planning permission for short lets?

Only a few UK areas have a planning rule that covers short lets across the area: the City of Edinburgh and part of Highland (control areas), Greater London (over 90 nights a year) and, in Wales, Eryri National Park (Article 4 direction). Elsewhere your council decides case by case whether a let is a material change of use, and some councils have local policies. Of the 114 council areas below, 5 have an area rule in force (in all or part), 24 have a local planning policy in force and 9 have a proposal or review under way.

The national rules behind the table

  • England: no short-let use class (the C5 proposal is not law). GOV.UK says your local planning authority decides, based on how the property is used and its impact on neighbours. Greater London: more than 90 nights a calendar year needs permission (Deregulation Act 2015, s44). England planning guide.
  • Scotland: councils can designate short-term let control areas. Inside one, a new short-term let very likely needs planning permission; outside, you may still need it if the let is a material change of use (mygov.scot). Scotland rules.
  • Wales: use classes C3 (main home), C5 (second home) and C6 (short-term let) since Oct 2022; changes between them are permitted development unless an Article 4 direction applies (WSI 2022/994). Wales rules.
  • Northern Ireland: no council pages yet. Belfast City Council is consulting (to 16 Nov 2026) on draft planning guidance for short-term lets; elsewhere, check with your council planning office. Northern Ireland rules.

Common questions

Do I need planning permission for a short-term let in the UK?

It depends on where the property is. In England there is no short-let use class, so your council decides case by case whether letting a whole home is a material change of use, and in Greater London letting a whole home for more than 90 nights a calendar year needs permission. In Scotland, a whole dwelling that is not your main home in a short-term let control area very likely needs permission. In Wales, moving between main home (C3), second home (C5) and short-term let (C6) is permitted development unless an Article 4 direction applies.

Which UK councils have short-term let control areas?

Scotland has two control areas in force: the whole City of Edinburgh and the Badenoch and Strathspey ward of Highland (mygov.scot). Argyll and Bute is consulting on one, and Highland has sent proposals for Inverness City and Highland Rural control areas to Scottish Ministers; these are not in force.

Where in Wales do you need planning permission to turn a home into a holiday let?

Inside Eryri (Snowdonia) National Park, an Article 4 direction in force since 1 June 2025 means changing a main home into a second home or short-term let needs planning permission. Cyngor Gwynedd's own direction for the rest of Gwynedd was quashed by the High Court. We found no other Welsh Article 4 direction on short lets among the councils we cover.

Does Blackpool need planning permission for holiday lets?

Blackpool Council says most Airbnb-style holiday lets need planning permission, because self-contained holiday flats and houses with no on-site management are a sui generis use, and that outside the Town Centre, Resort Core and six Holiday Accommodation Areas permission generally will not be granted.
