England

No national register yet (now expected March 2027). Use class C5 is still only a proposal. London has a 90-night cap. Business rates need 140 nights available and 70 nights let.

TopicStatusRuleOfficial source
Registration schemeNot yet in forceA mandatory national registration scheme is coming under s.228 of the Levelling-up and Regeneration Act 2023. GOV.UK (updated 2 Oct 2026) now says it is expected to begin in March 2027. No regulations, portal or fee have been published yet.source source
Planning use class (C5)Proposal onlyThe C5 short-term-let use class was consulted on in 2023 but is not law. Your local planning authority decides whether your letting is a material change of use. Councils can use Article 4 directions.source
London 90-night ruleIn forceIn Greater London, a whole home can be short-let for up to 90 nights per calendar year without planning permission. Going over that needs permission (Deregulation Act 2015, s.44).source
Business rates vs council taxIn forceTo be rated as a business, the let must have been available for at least 140 nights and actually let for at least 70 nights in the last 12 months, and you must intend to make it available for 140 nights in the next 12. From 24 Jul 2026 there are easements for owners of 5+ lets on the same property, and for lets sharing land with another business.source
Overnight Visitor LevyAnnounced, not yet lawOn 10 Sep 2026 the government announced that mayors and Foundation Strategic Authority leaders will get the power to introduce an Overnight Visitor Levy, charged as a percentage of the accommodation cost (not a flat fee). Local leaders choose whether to use it and can offer exemptions (e.g. campsites). Accommodation providers would pay it to the authority. A bill will be introduced in due course; no levy applies yet.source
Tax (FHL abolished)In forceThe FHL regime was abolished from April 2025.source
SafetyIn forceFire safety duties under the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005, plus gas and electrical safety, an EPC where needed, and insurance (see GOV.UK guidance).source
