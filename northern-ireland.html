Northern Ireland short-let rules: quick answers

Do I need a Tourism NI certificate to let my property in Northern Ireland?

In force. Under the Tourism (NI) Order 1992, all visitor accommodation needs a current Tourism NI certificate after an inspection. Certificates last 4 years. Star grading is voluntary.

Does a holiday let in Northern Ireland pay business rates?

In force. Self-contained self-catering available commercially for 140 days or more a year is non-domestic (rateable).

How are holiday lets taxed in Northern Ireland now the FHL regime has ended?

In force. The FHL regime was abolished UK-wide from April 2025. From April 2027, property income (including holiday-let profits) gets its own income tax rates: 22% basic, 42% higher, 47% additional, with finance cost relief given at 22% (GOV.UK, published 26 Nov 2025).

Do I need planning permission for a short-term let in Northern Ireland?

Belfast consultation to 16 Nov 2026. Belfast City Council is consulting (24 Aug to Mon 16 Nov 2026) on draft supplementary planning guidance for short-term lets, covering new-build and change of use of existing buildings (serviced apartments, apart-hotels, self-catering and holiday lets). The council says the draft is a material consideration in deciding planning applications. Elsewhere in NI, check with your council planning office.
