Rules by nation

Scotland

Every short-term let needs a council licence before taking bookings or guests. Control areas in Edinburgh and Badenoch & Strathspey. Visitor levies arriving city by city.

England

No national register yet (now expected March 2027). Use class C5 is still only a proposal. London has a 90-night cap. Business rates need 140 nights available and 70 nights let.

  • Registration schemeNot yet in force
  • Planning use class (C5)Proposal only
  • London 90-night ruleIn force
  • Business rates vs council taxIn force
See England rules →

Wales

Free registration with the Welsh Revenue Authority is mandatory by 31 March 2027. Councils can start a visitor levy (£1.30 per person per night); only Cardiff has so far. Licensing is legislated for, with a default start of March 2030. Business rates need 182 days let.

  • Registration (WRA)In force
  • Visitor levyCouncil-by-council
  • LicensingLegislated, not in force
  • 182-day rule (rates)In force (verified)
See Wales rules →

Northern Ireland

All tourist accommodation must hold a Tourism NI certificate. Inspections every 4 years. Fee £40 per unit (min £40, max £350).

  • CertificationIn force

Latest changes

  • 2026-10-25Wales

    Added council pages for Caerphilly (100% second-home council tax premium since April 2025, still charged in 2026/27; long-term empty premium only after 2 years, up to 300% after 5) and Torfaen (no second-home premium: one of only two Welsh councils not charging one in 2026/27, though the council says it is considering premiums subject to consultation). Neither has a visitor levy or an Article 4 direction on short lets; WRA registration still applies by 31 Mar 2027.

  • 2026-10-25Wales

    Added council pages for Neath Port Talbot (100% second-home council tax premium since April 2025, plus a new 6-month premium exception from April 2026 for self-catering lets moved off business rates onto council tax) and Rhondda Cynon Taf (100% second-home premium since April 2024). Neither has a visitor levy or an Article 4 direction; WRA registration still applies by 31 Mar 2027.

  • 2026-10-25Wales

    Added council pages for Bridgend (second-home council tax premium rose to 200%, a triple bill, from April 2026, among the highest in Wales) and Newport (100% second-home premium since April 2025, which the council says can include holiday lets on council tax). Neither has a visitor levy or an Article 4 direction; WRA registration still applies by 31 Mar 2027.

  • 2026-10-25Wales

    Added council pages for Wrexham (50% second-home council tax premium, one of the lowest in Wales, unchanged since 2017) and Flintshire (100% second-home premium since April 2023, kept for 2026/27). Neither has a visitor levy or an Article 4 direction; WRA registration still applies by 31 Mar 2027.
