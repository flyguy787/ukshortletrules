Scotland short-let rules: quick answers

Do I need a licence to run a short-term let in Scotland?

In force. All short-term lets (secondary letting, home letting, home sharing) need a licence from the local council before taking bookings or receiving guests. Operating without a licence is a criminal offence (Edinburgh notes fines up to £2,500).

Do I need planning permission for a short-term let in Scotland?

In force. Two control areas: the whole City of Edinburgh (since 5 Sep 2022) and Badenoch & Strathspey ward, Highland (since 4 Mar 2024). In these areas, using a whole dwelling that isn't your main home as a short-term let very likely needs planning permission. How are holiday lets taxed in Scotland now the FHL regime has ended?

In force. The UK-wide Furnished Holiday Let tax regime was abolished from April 2025. Income is now taxed as ordinary property income.

When does a short-term let in Scotland pay business rates instead of council tax?

In force (verified). To pay non-domestic rates instead of council tax, a let must not be anyone's only or main home, must be available commercially for at least 140 nights in the financial year (1 Apr to 31 Mar), and must actually be let for at least 70 nights. Send evidence to the assessor by the later of 56 days after the year end (usually 26 May) or 56 days after their request. If you miss the deadline you pay council tax for that year. Lets to friends or family don't count. Small Business Bonus Scheme relief may apply.
