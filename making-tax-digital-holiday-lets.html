Making Tax Digital for holiday lets

Making Tax Digital (MTD) for Income Tax means keeping digital records and sending quarterly updates through compatible software if your qualifying income is over HMRC's thresholds. Holiday-let income counts as property income. Here is who starts when, what counts, the quarterly dates, and a free qualifying-income checker.

In short: Making Tax Digital for holiday lets

From HMRC GOV.UK guidance. Not tax advice.

  • Who: sole traders and landlords registered for Self Assessment with self-employment and/or property income (HMRC).
  • Thresholds: over £50,000 in 2024–25 → from 6 Apr 2026; over £30,000 in 2025–26 → from 6 Apr 2027; over £20,000 in 2026–27 → from 6 Apr 2028.
  • Holiday lets count: gross holiday-let turnover is property income (FHL abolished Apr 2025) and forms part of qualifying income before expenses.
  • Do now: run the qualifying-income checker, confirm with HMRC's check tool, and get MTD alerts. Today (7 Nov) is the second quarterly update deadline for landlords already on MTD.

1. Thresholds and start dates

You need Making Tax Digital for Income Tax if you are a sole trader or landlord registered for Self Assessment, you get self-employment and/or property income, and your qualifying income is over the threshold for the relevant tax year (HMRC, updated 26 Mar 2026).

Tax yearQualifying incomeStart using MTDOfficial source
2024 to 2025Over £50,000From 6 April 2026 (you can still sign up)source
2025 to 2026Over £30,000From 6 April 2027source
2026 to 2027Over £20,000From 6 April 2028source
  • Partnerships will need MTD later; HMRC has not set that timeline yet.
  • You still need a Self Assessment return for the tax year before you start MTD.
  • To sign up you must be registered for Self Assessment and have submitted a tax return in the last 2 years (HMRC; sign-up guidance).

2. What counts as qualifying income

Qualifying income is total gross income from self-employment and property before expenses (turnover), not profit (HMRC, updated 11 Sep 2026).

  • Holiday-let income counts as property income. The FHL regime was abolished from April 2025, so former furnished holiday lets are taxed as ordinary property income and still feed into this total. See holiday let tax after the FHL abolition.
  • Joint property: count only your share (HMRC example: £50,000 income split 50/50 → £25,000 each).
  • Does not count: PAYE employment, partnership profit share as an individual partner, dividends (including from your own company), State Pension, private pensions, REIT/PAIF income, qualifying care relief, and basis-period transition profits.
  • Income from a source you have ceased still counts if another self-employment or property source continues. If all self-employment and property sources ceased before 6 April 2026, you do not need MTD for Income Tax.
  • Property income with a short accounting period: you must annualise the figure yourself before comparing it with the threshold.

3. Quarterly update deadlines

Quarterly updates are summaries of your digital records sent by compatible software. They are not full tax returns (HMRC).

Standard period (tax year)Update due by
6 Apr to 5 Jul7 August
6 Apr to 5 Oct7 November
6 Apr to 5 Jan7 February
6 Apr to 5 Apr7 May (next year)

If you use calendar periods (1 April to 31 March accounting), the same four deadlines apply: 7 August, 7 November, 7 February and 7 May.

  • 2026 to 2027 tax year: no penalty points for late quarterly updates. You still need to send the updates before your software tax return. Penalties for late Self Assessment returns still apply.
  • After 2026–27: a late quarterly update can give a penalty point; four points lead to a £200 penalty.
  • 7 November 2026 is the second quarterly update deadline (period 6 April to 5 October) for landlords already using MTD.

4. What hosts should do now

  • Work out your qualifying income for 2024–25, 2025–26 and 2026–27 with the checker, then confirm on GOV.UK.
  • If you are already in scope from 6 April 2026, send any outstanding quarterly updates through your software and note the 7 Nov / 7 Feb / 7 May deadlines.
  • If you start from 6 April 2027 or 2028, choose compatible software in good time and sign up when HMRC says you can.
  • Keep digital records of holiday-let income and expenses from the start of the tax year you join.
  • If you think you are digitally excluded or otherwise exempt, read HMRC's exemption guidance and apply before your start date. Exempt hosts still file a Self Assessment return.

Common questions

Do holiday lets count for Making Tax Digital for Income Tax?

Yes. Holiday-let income is property income. The furnished holiday lettings (FHL) tax regime ended from April 2025, so holiday-let income is treated like other property income and counts towards your qualifying income for Making Tax Digital.

What is qualifying income?

Your total gross income from self-employment and from property, before expenses (turnover). It is not your profit. Joint property income counts only as your share. PAYE employment, dividends (including from your own company), pensions, REIT/PAIF income and partnership profit share as an individual partner do not count.

When do I have to start Making Tax Digital?

If your qualifying income was over £50,000 in 2024 to 2025, you should have started from 6 April 2026 (you can still sign up). Over £30,000 in 2025 to 2026 means from 6 April 2027. Over £20,000 in 2026 to 2027 means from 6 April 2028. Partnerships have a later timeline that HMRC has not set yet.

What are the quarterly update deadlines?

For the standard tax-year periods: 6 April to 5 July due by 7 August; 6 April to 5 October due by 7 November; 6 April to 5 January due by 7 February; 6 April to 5 April due by 7 May the next year. Calendar accounting periods (1 April to 31 March) use the same four deadlines. Quarterly updates are summaries of digital records sent by software, not a full tax return.

Are there penalties for late quarterly updates?

For the 2026 to 2027 tax year there are no penalty points for late quarterly updates, but you still need to send the updates before your software tax return. Late Self Assessment return penalties still apply. After 2026 to 2027, a late quarterly update can give a penalty point; four points lead to a £200 penalty.

Do I still need a Self Assessment return?

Yes. You still need to submit a Self Assessment tax return for the tax year before you start Making Tax Digital. To sign up you must be registered for Self Assessment and have submitted a tax return in the last 2 years.

What if I am digitally excluded or think I am exempt?

Some people are exempt, for example if they are digitally excluded. Exempt people still report income and gains on a Self Assessment tax return. Check HMRC's exemption guidance on GOV.UK and apply before you need to start if you think an exemption applies.

Is this tax advice?

No. This page is general information from HMRC and GOV.UK guidance, checked on 7 November 2026. Use HMRC's official check tool and speak to an accountant or HMRC about your own figures before acting.
