Second homes council tax premium by council (UK)

How much council tax do second homes pay in each council?

Most councils now charge a second homes premium. In England councils can add up to 100% (double council tax) from 1 April 2025, and 64 of the 66 English councils below do, with one more starting in April 2027. Welsh councils can add up to 300%: of the 22 Welsh councils below, 6 charge more than 100%, 12 charge 100%, 2 charge less, 1 charges none and 1 is unclear. Scottish councils have set their own rates since 1 April 2026, when the 100% cap was removed: of the 31 Scottish councils below, 7 charge more than 100%, 22 charge 100%, 1 has agreed a rise and 1 is unclear. Holiday lets that pay business rates instead of council tax do not pay it.

At a glance

  • 119 council areas in this table: 66 in England, 22 in Wales and 31 in Scotland.
  • 98 charge a 100% premium (double council tax).
  • 13 charge more than 100% (6 in Wales, 7 in Scotland; 110% to 200%).
  • 2 charge less than 100% (Powys 75%, Wrexham 50%).
  • 2 have no premium, 2 have agreed a new or higher premium that has not started yet (Stratford-on-Avon from 1 Apr 2027 and City of Edinburgh from 1 Jan 2027) and 2 are unclear.

The national rules behind the table

  • England: from 1 April 2025 councils can charge up to 100% extra on second homes (furnished, nobody's sole or main residence), and must decide at least a year before it starts. Exceptions Classes E to L include annexes, homes marketed for sale or let (12 months) and holiday-only or seasonal homes restricted by planning (MHCLG guidance). A let valued for business rates (140 nights available, 70 let) does not pay it. England premium guide.
  • Wales: since 1 April 2023 councils can charge up to 300% (Welsh Government guidance). A self-catering let stays on business rates only if it is available for 252 nights and let for 182. Class 6 excepts homes a planning condition limits to holiday use. Wales premium guide.
  • Scotland: premiums of up to 100% (double) started on 1 April 2024. The Housing (Scotland) Act 2025 removed that cap: from 1 April 2026 the default is still a 100% increase, but each council can set a higher or lower rate, or none, and can vary it by area or type of home (SSI 2026/169; Scottish Government circular 6/2026). A second home is furnished, nobody's sole or main residence and lived in for at least 25 days in 12 months. The Scottish Government's 2026-27 return lists each council's rate (gov.scot data); we checked every council against its own page too, as some have changed since. A self-catering let that qualifies for business rates does not pay council tax. Scotland business rates or council tax checker.
  • Northern Ireland: no second homes premium is listed on this site; we have no council pages there yet. Northern Ireland rules.

Common questions

What is the second homes council tax premium?

It is extra council tax a council can charge on a second home: a home that is substantially furnished but is nobody's sole or main residence. Each council decides whether to charge it and at what rate. In England the maximum is 100% (double council tax) from 1 April 2025, and in Wales it is up to 300% (four times) since 1 April 2023. In Scotland premiums of up to 100% started on 1 April 2024; since 1 April 2026 there is no upper limit, the default is 100% and each council sets its own rate.

Do holiday lets pay the second homes premium?

Only if the let is on council tax. A holiday let valued for business rates does not pay council tax, so the premium does not apply. In England that means available for at least 140 nights and actually let for at least 70 nights in the last 12 months; in Wales, at least 252 nights available and 182 let; in Scotland, at least 140 nights available and 70 let in the financial year. Lets that miss the test move to council tax, where the premium can apply unless an exception fits.

Which councils charge the highest second homes premium?

Among the 119 councils in this table, the highest current rate is 200% (a bill of 300%), charged by Bridgend in Wales and by Aberdeenshire, East Dunbartonshire, Glasgow City, Highland and West Dunbartonshire in Scotland. City of Edinburgh has decided on 300% (four times council tax) from 1 January 2027, and Scottish Borders' policy reaches 200% from 1 April 2028. Conwy, Ceredigion, Denbighshire and Gwynedd charge 150%. Midlothian says graduated premiums of up to 500% will normally apply to second homes too, but this is unclear. Every English council in the table that charges the premium charges the 100% maximum.

Which councils do not charge a second homes premium?

In this table, Ribble Valley (England) and Torfaen (Wales) charge no second homes premium in 2026/27 — Ribble Valley still gives a 10% discount on unoccupied furnished homes. Stratford-on-Avon has agreed a 100% premium that starts on 1 April 2027. Blaenau Gwent and Midlothian are unclear because their sources disagree, so ask the council. Every Scottish council in the table charges at least 100%.
