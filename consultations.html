Short-let consultations: open and closing soon

Public consultations that could change the rules for short-term lets and holiday lets in England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland, with closing dates and how to respond. Every detail comes from the official consultation page, linked on each one. Checked 4 Nov 2026.

Open now: 6 consultations

2 close within 14 days. The next closes on Sun 15 Nov 2026: Proposed short-term let control area (Mull and Iona; Coll and Tiree; Islay, Jura and Colonsay; Oban, Lorn and the Inner Isles) (Argyll and Bute Council).

What happens after the open consultations close:

  • Argyll and Bute (planning control area, closes 15 Nov): findings go to a future council meeting; if approved, the Scottish Government would have to agree before it could start
  • Falkirk (visitor levy, closes 25 Nov): Full Council expected to decide in January 2027
  • Dundee (visitor levy, closes 27 Nov): earliest a levy could apply is 2028/29
  • Highland (visitor levy, closes 30 Nov): councillors could decide on 10 December 2026
  • Moray (second homes council tax, closes 14 Dec): report to Full Council in January 2027

Proposed short-term let control area (Mull and Iona; Coll and Tiree; Islay, Jura and Colonsay; Oban, Lorn and the Inner Isles) 10 days left

Who is consulting
Argyll and Bute Council
Where
Argyll and Bute (Scotland)
Topic
Planning control area
Opened
Mon 21 Sep 2026
Closes
Sun 15 Nov 2026
How to respond
Online consultation form on the council website.

In a control area, planning permission would always be needed for a new change of use of a home (including flats) to a short-term let. The council says it would only affect new secondary-letting proposals, not existing operators, and the Scottish Government would have to agree before it could be introduced. The consultation was extended by two weeks to 15 November after the council corrected a table in its materials.

Planning guidance

not stated (announced 24 Aug 2026 as a 12-week consultation)

How to respond
On the council's Your Say platform, by email to localdevelopmentplan@belfastcity.gov.uk, or by post to Local Development Plan, Belfast Planning Service, Cecil Ward Building, 4-10 Linenhall Street, Belfast BT2 8BP. Submissions after 5pm on 16 November will not be considered.

Non-statutory guidance supporting Policy HOU13 (short-term let accommodation) in the Belfast Local Development Plan. The council says it will be a material consideration in deciding planning applications for short-term lets, both new-build and change of use.

Visitor Levy: Have Your Say 22 days left

Who is consulting
Dundee City Council
Where
Dundee (Scotland)
Topic
Visitor levy
Opened
Mon 31 Aug 2026
Closes
Fri 27 Nov 2026
How to respond
Online survey (about 10 minutes) linked from the council page. Paper copies at The McManus and the Central Library. Help: 01382 549946.

Two options: 5% of the room price per night, or a fixed £5 per room per night. You can also say there should be no levy. The council says the earliest a levy could apply is 2028/29.

Statutory consultation on a tiered fixed-amount visitor levy 25 days left

Who is consulting
The Highland Council
Where
Highland (Scotland)
Topic
Visitor levy
Opened
Mon 7 Sep 2026
Closes
Mon 30 Nov 2026
How to respond
Engage Highland portal. Paper forms from local libraries and service points, by phone on 01349 781020 or by email to visitorlevyconsultation@highland.gov.uk.

Proposed £5 per room or area per night for hotels, B&Bs, short-term lets and similar, and £2 for hostels, campsites and caravan parks, charged for nine months of the year (not December to February). Councillors could decide on 10 December 2026; an 18-month lead-in would follow.

Long-term empty properties and second homes: council tax charges 39 days left

Who is consulting
Moray Council
Where
Moray (Scotland)
Topic
Second homes council tax
Opened
Fri 30 Oct 2026
Closes
Mon 14 Dec 2026
How to respond
Survey on Moray Engage. Hard copies from Information Hubs in libraries across Moray. Questions: CtxEnq@moray.gov.uk.

Asks how far the current 100% second home premium (and the 100% charge on long-term empty homes) should rise, now councils can go above 100% from 1 April 2026, and what reliefs to offer. It does not consider cutting the charges. A report goes to Full Council in January 2027.
