Wales visitor accommodation licensing: what the 2026 Act means for hosts

Do I need a licence for a holiday let in Wales?

Not yet. The Development of Tourism and Regulation of Visitor Accommodation (Wales) Act 2026 will require a licence for self-contained self-catering lets, but its licensing parts are not in force: the legal default start is 4 March 2030 (GOV.WALES). What you must do now is register with the Welsh Revenue Authority by 31 March 2027; that registration will be a condition of the licence.

In short

From the 2026 Act on legislation.gov.uk and GOV.WALES. Not legal advice.

  • No licence needed yet. The 2026 Act received Royal Assent on 27 April 2026, but its licensing parts are not in force. The legal default start is 4 March 2030; the Welsh Ministers can bring it in earlier or move it back by up to 2 years.
  • Who: self-contained self-catering lets (beds, toilet, sink, bath or shower and a kitchen for guests only) let for 31 nights or less. Hotels, B&Bs, guesthouses, hostels, bunkhouses and licensed campsites are excluded at the start.
  • Issued by the Welsh Ministers, not your council. A licence lasts 1 year unless regulations set a different period.
  • Five specific standards: a written fire risk assessment, an electrical condition report (valid up to 5 years), a gas maintenance record, a carbon monoxide alarm where there is a gas, oil or solid fuel appliance, and public liability insurance. Plus training, WRA registration and steps to prevent nuisance to neighbours.
  • Fees and fines not set yet. The first fee regulations need at least 12 weeks of consultation. Once in force, letting without a licence is an offence with a fine; the Act sets no amount.
  • Do now: register with the Welsh Revenue Authority by 31 March 2027 (free; penalties start at £100 per premises), see what to do now vs 2030, and get an email when the rules change.

Two different Welsh Acts. The licensing law is the Development of Tourism and Regulation of Visitor Accommodation (Wales) Act 2026 (2026 asc 10; in Welsh, Deddf Datblygu Twristiaeth a Rheoleiddio Llety Ymwelwyr (Cymru) 2026). The register and visitor levy law is the Visitor Accommodation (Register and Levy) Etc. (Wales) Act 2025 (2025 asc 5). The 2026 Act builds on the 2025 register.

Who will need a licence?

The licence is for “regulated visitor accommodation”. Under section 5, at the start that means self-catering accommodation that is self-contained, in a building, or in a mobile home, vessel or other vehicle that is permanently or semi-permanently in one place. It is “self-contained” if it has:

  • one or more beds;
  • a toilet, a sink, and a bath or shower;
  • a kitchen or kitchen area;

and those facilities are for the guests’ exclusive use during their stay. It must also be offered on a short-term basis (stays of 31 nights or less, under section 2(3) of the 2025 Act) for business or leisure travel or educational trips.

Not covered at the start (section 5(2)): self-catering in or at a hotel or aparthotel, guesthouse or B&B; a youth hostel, bunkhouse or camping barn; a licensed campsite or a caravan site licensed under Part 1 of the Caravan Sites and Control of Development Act 1960; and touring vehicles that are not kept in one place. In our reading, a room in your own home where guests share your kitchen or bathroom is not “self-contained”, so it is not covered unless later regulations add it. The Welsh Ministers can add other types of accommodation by regulations after at least 12 weeks of consultation (section 5(1)(b) and (6)). GOV.WALES describes the scheme as “starting with self-catering accommodation”.

The licence is held by the visitor accommodation provider, which the Act defines as a person registered with the Welsh Revenue Authority under the 2025 Act. Licences are granted by the Welsh Ministers, not your council (sections 14 and 20). The Act lets the Welsh Ministers share information with councils, fire and rescue authorities, the Welsh Revenue Authority and National Park authorities (section 36).

What a licence will require

Every licence comes with conditions set by section 15:

  • WRA registration for the premises and the accommodation.
  • Provider training: training the Welsh Ministers will prescribe by regulations (section 16).
  • Fitness standards: the specific standards below, and the general standard during every stay.
  • Neighbours: take all reasonable steps to prevent guest conduct that could cause nuisance or annoyance to people living nearby (limited to what is foreseeable and practicable to prevent).
  • Guest information: take reasonable steps to give guests, before they stay, a copy of the licence certificate and a statement of information; keep a copy of the certificate at the property (paper or electronic).

The Welsh Ministers can add further conditions by regulations (section 17), after consultation.

The five specific standards (sections 9 to 13)

StandardWhat the Act says
Fire safety (s9)Keep a written fire risk assessment and a record of the general fire precautions identified and taken; review it and update it after significant changes.
Electrical (s10)Hold a valid electrical condition report from a qualified person, valid for up to 5 years (or sooner if the report says so), with a written record of any remedial work.
Gas (s11)A gas maintenance record for gas appliances and pipework on, or serving, the premises.
Carbon monoxide (s12)A working carbon monoxide alarm in any room with a gas appliance, an oil-fired appliance or a solid fuel appliance.
Insurance (s13)“A public liability insurance policy is in place in relation to liabilities to third parties”; the cover and amount can be set by regulations.

The general fitness standard (section 7) covers safety (structurally stable, well maintained, working gas, electricity and water, free from carbon monoxide, fire and other hazards, hygienic, secure) and amenity (adequate lighting, heating, ventilation, drainage, sleeping space, kitchen facilities and toilets). Guests will also be able to rely on fitness as a term of their booking contract (sections 38 and 39).

Licence length, provisional licences and appeals

  • A licence lasts 1 year from the date it is granted unless regulations set a different period, and must stay in effect while a renewal is being decided (section 25).
  • If you are registered and trained but can’t yet show the property meets the fitness standards (for example, during works), you can apply for a provisional licence, which does not allow guests to stay (section 27).
  • You can appeal a decision to refuse or revoke a licence to the First-tier Tribunal within 28 days (section 29).

Fees and penalties

  • Licence fees: not set yet. The Welsh Ministers may set fees by regulations, and must consult for at least 12 weeks before the first fee regulations (section 35).
  • Letting without a licence (once in force) is an offence if done in the course of trade or business, unless you hold the licence, have the licence holder’s permission or have a reasonable excuse. The penalty is a fine on summary conviction; the Act sets no amount (section 32).
  • Adverts: anyone advertising visitor accommodation in Wales (licensed or not) will have to include the WRA registration number and how to look the premises up in the public directory, and must not advertise regulated accommodation in the course of business unless it is licensed. Both are offences with a fine, with a reasonable excuse defence (sections 43 and 44).
  • Penalty notices: the Welsh Ministers can offer a penalty notice instead of prosecution; the sum will be set by regulations (section 55).
  • Obstructing an inspector and giving false or misleading information are also offences (sections 33 and 54).

Many of the specific standards match good practice you may already follow (a written fire risk assessment, an electrical report, gas safety checks, carbon monoxide alarms and public liability cover). See holiday let insurance by nation.

How it fits with WRA registration, the levy and the 182-night rule

  • WRA registration (2025 Act) is in force now: if you take bookings of 31 nights or less you must register by 31 March 2027, even if your council has no levy (GOV.WALES). It is free. Missing it can mean a penalty of £100 per premises, then £10 a day per premises for up to 30 days, then a further £1,000 per premises (WRA penalties guidance). Registration is a licence condition, and the WRA register and the licence register will feed one public directory of Welsh visitor accommodation (section 42).
  • Visitor levy (2025 Act): councils choose whether to charge it, from 1 April or 1 October, after consultation and a year’s notice (WRA guide). The rate is £1.30 per person per night, or 75p for shared rooms in hostels, bunkhouses, bothies or dormitories and for tent pitches (GOV.WALES). Cardiff’s applies to stays from 1 April 2027. The licensing Act does not set or change the levy.
  • 182-night rule: to be rated for business rates, a Welsh self-catering let must be available for at least 252 nights and actually let for at least 182 nights in 12 months; from 1 April 2026 you can average over 2 or 3 years and count up to 14 nights donated to a registered charity (GOV.UK). Otherwise council tax applies, and the council may charge a second homes premium. The Welsh Government is consulting on these criteria until 30 October 2026 (GOV.WALES). We found nothing in the licensing Act that changes them, and the Act’s licence test is the type of accommodation, not how many nights you let.

Not set yet

These will come in regulations or guidance, and none has been published as of 27 Oct 2026: licence and training fees; the training content; any extra licence conditions or approval requirements; the minimum insurance cover; penalty notice amounts; the application process; and any exemptions (section 51). Not verified Whether the Welsh Ministers will bring the scheme in earlier than March 2030, or in stages, has not been announced.

Common questions

Do I need a licence for my holiday let in Wales now?

No. The Development of Tourism and Regulation of Visitor Accommodation (Wales) Act 2026 received Royal Assent on 27 April 2026, but the licensing parts are not in force yet. Under section 61 they start on 4 March 2030 unless the Welsh Ministers bring them in earlier or move that date back by up to 2 years. GOV.WALES says the legal default is March 2030, with consultation on the regulations and guidance first. What you must do now is register with the Welsh Revenue Authority by 31 March 2027.

What is the exact name of the Welsh licensing law?

The Development of Tourism and Regulation of Visitor Accommodation (Wales) Act 2026 (2026 asc 10). Its Welsh title is Deddf Datblygu Twristiaeth a Rheoleiddio Llety Ymwelwyr (Cymru) 2026. It is a different Act from the Visitor Accommodation (Register and Levy) Etc. (Wales) Act 2025 (2025 asc 5), which created the Welsh Revenue Authority register and the visitor levy.

Which holiday lets will need a licence?

At the start, self-catering accommodation that is self-contained, in a building or in a mobile home, vessel or other vehicle that stays in one place, offered for stays of 31 nights or less for business or leisure travel or educational trips. Self-contained means it has beds, a toilet, a sink, a bath or shower and a kitchen or kitchen area, all for the guests' exclusive use during the stay. Hotels, aparthotels, guesthouses, B&Bs, hostels, bunkhouses, camping barns and licensed campsites and caravan sites are excluded. The Welsh Ministers can add other types of accommodation by regulations after at least 12 weeks of consultation.

Who issues the licence, my council or the Welsh Government?

The Welsh Ministers. Under the Act the Welsh Ministers grant, renew and revoke visitor accommodation licences and keep the register of licences. Councils do not grant the licences, although the Act lets the Welsh Ministers share information with councils, fire and rescue authorities, the Welsh Revenue Authority and National Park authorities.

How much will a Welsh visitor accommodation licence cost?

No fee has been set. Section 35 lets the Welsh Ministers set fees by regulations, and before the first fee regulations they must consult for at least 12 weeks. The training that licence holders must complete, and any fee for it, will also be set by regulations.

What standards will a licensed let have to meet?

A general fitness standard (safe, secure, hygienic, in good repair, with adequate heating, lighting, ventilation, drainage, sleeping space, kitchen facilities and toilets) and five specific standards: a written fire risk assessment and a record of the fire precautions taken, a valid electrical condition report (up to 5 years), a gas maintenance record, a working carbon monoxide alarm in any room with a gas, oil or solid fuel appliance, and public liability insurance. Licence holders must also be registered with the Welsh Revenue Authority, complete prescribed training, take reasonable steps to prevent nuisance to neighbours, and give guests a copy of the licence certificate and a statement of information.

What happens if I let without a licence after it starts?

Once section 32 is in force it will be an offence to provide regulated visitor accommodation in the course of trade or business without a licence (or the permission of the licence holder), unless you have a reasonable excuse. The Act says the penalty is a fine on summary conviction and does not set an amount. The Welsh Ministers can also offer a penalty notice instead of prosecution, with the sum set by regulations. Advertising regulated accommodation without a licence will also be an offence.

Is Welsh Revenue Authority registration the same as the licence?

No. Registration is a separate, free requirement under the 2025 Act: if you take bookings of 31 nights or less in Wales you must register by 31 March 2027, even if your council has no visitor levy, and penalties start at £100 per premises. Being registered for the premises will be a condition of getting and keeping a licence, so registering now is the first step towards licensing.

Does the licensing Act change the 182-night business rates rule or the visitor levy?

We found nothing in the Act that does. Its amendments are to the Development of Tourism Act 1969 and to the register provisions of the 2025 Act. The 182-night letting test for business rates and the council-by-council visitor levy are set under other law. The Welsh Government is separately consulting, until 30 October 2026, on the self-catering criteria for local tax.
