Northern Ireland short-term lets: how to get a Tourism NI certificate

Every holiday let in Northern Ireland, including Airbnbs and self-catering cottages, needs a current Tourism NI certificate. Here is how to get one, what the inspector checks, and a checklist you can tick off (it saves in your browser).

The law: the Tourism (Northern Ireland) Order 1992. Who certifies: Tourism NI, after an inspection. How long it lasts: 4 years. Self-catering fee: £40 per unit (minimum £40, maximum £350).

Step by step

  1. Check planning and building control. Ask your local council planning department early. Planning permission and building regulations can apply even without structural work.
  2. Get the basics in place: fire risk assessment, yearly Gas Safe check, electrical safety, public liability insurance, and your lender's OK.
  3. Apply to Tourism NI. Online at forms.tourismni.com with the fee, or by post. For a pack, email qa@tourismni.com.
  4. Book the inspection. An inspector contacts you once your form and fee arrive.
  5. Inspection day. The inspector checks the outside, the structure and safety, every room, the information you give guests, and how the unit is managed.
  6. Deal with any conditions. You agree a timeframe of at least 30 days. A follow-up visit, if needed, is free.
  7. Get your certificate, usually by post within a few days, and display it with your prices.
  8. Keep it current. Send a self-review statement in each of the 3 years between inspections, then get re-inspected every 4 years.

Common questions

Do I need a licence for an Airbnb in Northern Ireland?

Northern Ireland does not have a short-term let licence like Scotland. Instead, tourism legislation requires all tourist accommodation, including self-catering, to hold a current Tourism NI certificate, which you get after an inspection.

How much does Tourism NI certification cost?

For self-catering, Tourism NI charges £40 for each unit you plan to let, with a minimum fee of £40 and a maximum of £350. B&Bs, guest houses and guest accommodation pay £20 per bedroom (minimum £80, maximum £200).

How do I apply for a Tourism NI certificate?

Apply online at forms.tourismni.com with the fee, or download the paper form and post it. You can also ask the Quality and Standards team for an application pack (qa@tourismni.com). Once your form and fee arrive, an inspector contacts you to book a visit.

How long does a Tourism NI certificate last?

4 years. Tourism NI inspects every 4 years, and in each of the 3 years in between you send a short self-review statement online. Tourism NI can also make free ad hoc inspections, for example after a guest complaint.

What happens if the inspector finds problems?

The inspector talks you through the outcome. If there are conditions on your certificate, you agree a timeframe of not less than 30 days to do the work. Sometimes a follow-up visit is needed, usually within a month, and there is no charge for it.

Is star grading compulsory in Northern Ireland?

No. The Quality Star Rating Scheme (1 to 5 stars) is voluntary. Certification is the legal requirement; grading is optional.

Do I have to keep a guest register?

Yes. Tourism NI says you are legally required to record each visitor's name, address, dates of arrival and departure, and nationality.

Do I need planning permission for a short-term let in Northern Ireland?

It may be needed. Planning permission and building regulations can apply when you start a holiday let, convert or extend a property, or build a new one, and the two use different meanings of "change of use". Ask your local council planning department early.

Do I pay business rates on a holiday let in Northern Ireland?

Self-catering accommodation available for short-period lets for 140 days or more a year is non-domestic and pays business rates. Below 140 days, business rates do not apply. Land and Property Services handles rates.
