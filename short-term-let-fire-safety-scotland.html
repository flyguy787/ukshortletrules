Fire safety for short-term lets in Scotland: what you need and a checklist

Every short-term let in Scotland needs a licence, and fire safety is part of it. Here is what the law, the licence conditions and the Scottish Government's guidance for small self-catering lets say, in plain English, plus a checklist you can tick off (it saves in your browser).

The law: Part 3 of the Fire (Scotland) Act 2005 and the Fire Safety (Scotland) Regulations 2006. Licence conditions: Schedule 3 of the Licensing of Short-term Lets Order 2022. Guidance: Scottish Government, "Fire safety: existing premises with sleeping accommodation", Annex 2 for small B&B and self-catering premises (28 February 2022). Who enforces it: the Scottish Fire and Rescue Service (SFRS).

What the licence needs

  • When you apply, submit a fire safety checklist and confirm a fire risk assessment is in place. Some councils ask for a copy. Councils send checklists to SFRS for consultation.
  • Mandatory condition: satisfactory equipment for detecting and warning of fire or suspected fire, and of carbon monoxide at a concentration hazardous to health.
  • Mandatory condition: records showing upholstered furnishings and mattresses guests can use comply with the Furniture and Furnishings (Fire Safety) Regulations 1988. mygov.scot says labels, receipts or photos can prove this.
  • Also mandatory: an annual gas safety inspection (if there is a gas supply), an electrical inspection at least every five years with an EICR, a PAT report with date-labelled appliances, and a legionella risk assessment.
  • On display for guests: a certified copy of the licence and conditions, fire, gas and electrical safety information, how to call the emergency services, and copies of the gas safety report, EICR and PAT report.

Does Annex 2 cover your property?

Annex 2 is the simple guidance for self-catering premises for up to 10 people and B&Bs in the operator's home for up to 8 guests, where bedrooms escape through a traditional hall with at least one exit straight outside, there is no guest accommodation below the ground floor or above the first floor, it is not the guests' main home, and no storey is over 200 m². SFRS says premises outside Annex 2 may be higher risk, so ask your local fire safety enforcement team.

The fire risk assessment in 7 steps

  1. Who is at risk? Guests, cleaners, trades and visitors, noting children, elderly or disabled people.
  2. What could start a fire, and what could burn? Cooking, heaters, open fires, smoking, appliances; furniture, bedding, kindling, rubbish, flammable liquids, LPG.
  3. Evaluate the risk. How fast could fire spread (layers of wallpaper, polystyrene ceiling tiles, wood panelling)? Could a bin fire outside block an exit?
  4. Reduce the risk and add safety measures. Detection, holding back fire and smoke, easy escape, and a way to tackle a small fire.
  5. Make a plan for improvements with priorities and timescales.
  6. Record it, including how you'll review it, your fire action plan and maintenance arrangements.
  7. Review it regularly, for example after building work or if your guests change.

What a typical self-catering let needs (Annex 2 benchmarks)

These are benchmarks, not fixed minimums. Your risk assessment decides the final list.

More than three bedrooms: a smoke alarm in each bedroom too. Optical type in halls and landings.

Common questions

Do short-term lets in Scotland need a fire risk assessment?

Yes. Short-term lets used for sleeping accommodation are covered by Part 3 of the Fire (Scotland) Act 2005, and the person who controls the premises must make sure a fire risk assessment is carried out and kept under review. When you apply for a licence you submit a fire safety checklist and confirm a fire risk assessment is in place; some councils ask for a copy.

Can I do the fire risk assessment myself?

For small self-catering properties the Scottish Government guidance says the owner should usually be able to do it. The Scottish Fire and Rescue Service says you can do it only if you are competent; otherwise use a suitably qualified fire risk assessor. SFRS does not do assessments for you.

What fire safety is a mandatory licence condition in Scotland?

The licence holder must have satisfactory equipment for detecting and warning of fire or suspected fire, and of carbon monoxide at a level hazardous to health. They must also keep records showing upholstered furnishings and mattresses guests can use comply with the Furniture and Furnishings (Fire Safety) Regulations 1988.

Where do smoke and heat alarms go in a Scottish holiday let?

The Annex 2 benchmark for small self-catering lets is a smoke alarm in the hall (and the first floor landing in a two-storey property) within 3 m of every bedroom door and no more than 7.5 m from any part of the hall, a smoke alarm in each living room and separate dining room, and a heat alarm in the kitchen. With more than three bedrooms, add a smoke alarm in each bedroom. Alarms should be interlinked.

Do I need fire doors in a Scottish holiday cottage?

Not usually. Doors onto the escape route need not be fire doors but should hold back smoke and fire long enough to escape, for example a solid timber door fitting its frame with gaps of no more than 4 mm. In a two-storey property with more than three upstairs bedrooms, the ground floor doors onto the escape route should have self-closers.

Do I need a fire extinguisher or a fire blanket?

The Annex 2 benchmark for self-catering is a fire blanket in the kitchen. It does not mention extinguishers. Your own risk assessment decides whether you need more.

Do I need emergency lighting?

Not formal emergency lighting under the Annex 2 benchmark. If the escape route has no street light or other outside light, put plug-in night lights that keep working in a power cut in the hall and landing, and check they work at the start of each let.

Does Annex 2 cover my property?

It covers self-catering premises for up to 10 people, and B&Bs in the operator's home for up to 8 guests, where bedrooms escape through a traditional hall with at least one exit straight outside, there are no guest rooms below the ground floor or above the first floor, and no storey is over 200 square metres. Larger or unusual properties may be higher risk: ask your local SFRS fire safety enforcement team.

What gas and electrical checks does a Scottish licence need?

An annual gas safety inspection of all gas pipes, flues and appliances if there is a gas supply, and an electrical inspection by a competent person at least every five years with an Electrical Installation Condition Report, plus a Portable Appliance Testing report and date labels on movable appliances guests can use.

Who enforces fire safety in Scottish short-term lets?

The Scottish Fire and Rescue Service. It may visit, ask about your fire risk assessment and check your fire safety measures, and can take formal enforcement action where serious problems are not fixed. Councils issue the licences and send fire safety checklists to SFRS for consultation.
